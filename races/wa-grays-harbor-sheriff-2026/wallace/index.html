1 Voters’ Pamphlet Statement (General Election 2026)

Elected Experience

My name is Darrin Wallace, and I’m your current Grays Harbor County Sheriff.  I have served for 20 years at the Sheriff’s Office and have held positions as North Beach Resident Deputy, Detective, Patrol Sergeant, Detective Sergeant, Supervisor of the Region 3 Critical Incident Investigation Team for two years, Chief Investigations Deputy, and Sheriff.  I also served 20 years in the U. S. Coast Guard where I served in executive leadership positions such as the Commanding Officer of Station Grays Harbor and Executive Petty Officer of Station Golden Gate. My wife Brandi and I have a son named Tommy.

Statement

The criminal justice center project is still my #1 priority.  Our jail is over 45 years old and is in a constant state of disrepair.  These maintenance issues are a huge liability to Corrections Officers and Inmates alike.  A new jail would minimize potential catastrophic incidents from aging infrastructure and maintenance failures.

I plan on getting our jail accredited through the Washington Association of Sheriff’s and Police Chiefs (WASPC).  This will decrease susceptibility to lawsuits, lower insurance costs and increase public confidence in our office.

I plan on developing a sheriff cadet program to introduce young people to law enforcement through training, mentorship, and community service. Cadets will learn discipline, teamwork, leadership, and basic policing skills. The program builds confidence, responsibility, and career awareness, preparing participants for future employment with the sheriff’s office.

During the past 4 years, I have proven to be tough on crime and will continue to aggressively investigate crimes and arrest individuals responsible for those crimes.  This encompasses violent crimes, crimes against children, theft, and drug dealers selling drugs in our community.  I support the Second Amendment and the rights of the people to keep and bear arms and those rights shall not be infringed upon.

Finding 6.1: Top priority is an estimated $80 million jail replacement MODERATE

What happened
Wallace's stated top priority is replacing the 45-year-old county jail at an estimated cost of $80 million. The county has a median household income of $63,539 and a 15 percent poverty rate, and no funding plan for a project of that size has been made public.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Wallace says the jail needs replacement because of constant repairs and a vertical design that creates safety hazards for corrections staff, and has cited Skagit County's linear design as a model.
