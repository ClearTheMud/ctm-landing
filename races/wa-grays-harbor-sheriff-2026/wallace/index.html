Finding 5.1: Severe Budget Cuts Threaten Department Staffing HIGH

What happened
In 2024, Wallace warned publicly that the sheriff's department would have to cut 7 deputies if proposed county budget cuts proceeded. The department already faces staffing shortages — one patrol deputy, four to five corrections deputies, and four support specialists vacant. Budget constraints limit the sheriff's ability to deliver on public safety promises.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
High
Defense
Budget is set by the Board of County Commissioners, not the sheriff. Wallace has been vocal about the impact of cuts and has sought alternative funding. Secured $900,000 for body cameras despite budget pressures.

Finding 5.2: $80 Million Jail Replacement — Ambitious and Unfunded MODERATE

What happened
Wallace's top priority is replacing the 45-year-old county jail at an estimated cost of $80 million. In a county with a median household income of $63,539 and a 15% poverty rate, securing voter approval for this scale of capital project is a significant political challenge. No concrete funding plan has been publicly detailed.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
The facility genuinely requires replacement — constant repairs, vertical design creates safety hazards for corrections staff. Wallace cited Skagit County's linear design as a model. A new jail is a public safety necessity, not a discretionary project.
