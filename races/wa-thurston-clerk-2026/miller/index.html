Statement

The Clerk’s Office is a place where people interact directly with the justice system, often during some of the most stressful moments of their lives. Whether someone is filing for protection, navigating family law, addressing a court matter, or seeking records, they deserve accurate information, compassionate service, and an office that is open and accessible to the public.

I am running for Thurston County Clerk to restore public trust, reopen meaningful access to the office, and ensure people are treated with dignity and respect. A person’s ability to access court services should not depend on income, disability, language, family crisis, or whether they can afford an attorney. My nearly 30 years of public service have prepared me to lead with both accountability and compassion.

I will work collaboratively with staff, labor, the courts, attorneys, county leaders, and community partners to address backlogs, improve service delivery, restore helpful in-person service, and build a healthier workplace culture. When staff are supported and the office functions well, the public receives better service.

Thurston County deserves a Clerk’s Office that is professional, responsive, accurate, accessible, and people-centered. I am ready to lead that work and would be honored to earn your vote.

Finding 6.1: Terminated from Thurston County Racial Equity Program Manager Role MODERATE

What happened
Miller was fired from her position as Thurston County's first-ever Racial Equity Program Manager in January 2023 by County Manager Ramiro Chavez. Miller publicly alleged the termination was unfair. Chavez declined to explain the reasons for her departure. The firing was covered by The Daily Chronicle and KING 5 News, and drew criticism from community leaders on the county's Racial Equity and Inclusion Council. The specific cause of termination remains publicly undisclosed.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Miller would frame this as standing up for racial equity against an administration that was not truly committed to the work. Community leaders publicly supported her, with one calling her departure "alarming" and describing her as an engaging, community-oriented professional. In a solidly Democratic county, advocacy for racial equity may be viewed positively by primary voters.
