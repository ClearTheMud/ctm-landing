1 BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Rep. Marilyn Strickland (D) is a 3-term incumbent in a safely Democratic district (D+9, margins increasing each cycle). She faces a progressive primary challenge from Adam Arafat, a 20-year Army veteran running on “People Over Donors” anti-AIPAC/anti-corporate-PAC messaging. Strickland holds a >100:1 fundraising advantage ($1.16M vs. ~$0 reported). Washington uses a top-two primary — all candidates appear on a single ballot, and the top two vote-getters advance to the general regardless of party. The progressive anti-incumbent vote is split between Arafat and DSA-affiliated Alex Scheel.

4 Key Race Dynamics

Financial asymmetry
Strickland holds a >100:1 fundraising advantage over all challengers combined. She has raised $1.16M; no challenger has reported any financial activity to the FEC.
Progressive vote split
The anti-incumbent progressive vote divides between Arafat (grassroots/veteran lane) and Scheel (DSA/activist lane), virtually guaranteeing neither can consolidate enough support to threaten a top-two finish.
AIPAC as attack surface
AIPAC PAC is Strickland’s #1 PAC donor at $65,300. Both Arafat and Scheel have positioned their campaigns against AIPAC influence. Nationally, AIPAC-focused primary challenges have succeeded in other districts (Bowman NY-16, Bush MO-01 in 2024) — but those races featured funded, endorsed, digitally-capable challengers.
Top-two primary insulation
Washington’s top-two system means all 6 candidates compete on a single ballot. With the only Republican (Chung) consolidating the R vote by default, and the progressive vote splitting 2+ ways, Strickland is structurally insulated from a competitive primary outcome.
Military corridor
JBLM is the district’s economic engine. Strickland has secured $135M in NDAA provisions and authored bipartisan military-family legislation signed into law. This creates a deliverables-based incumbency advantage that is difficult for any challenger to counter.
