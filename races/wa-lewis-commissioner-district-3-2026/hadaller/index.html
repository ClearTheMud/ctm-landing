Finding 6.1: Dual Office Question — PUD Commissioner Seeking County Commissioner MODERATE

What happened
Hadaller currently serves as Lewis County PUD Commissioner for District 3 while simultaneously running for Lewis County Commissioner District 3. If elected, it is unclear whether he would resign his PUD seat or attempt to hold both positions. Washington state law (RCW 42.20.010) generally prohibits holding two "incompatible" public offices. Even if legally permissible, holding two elected positions covering the same geographic area raises governance and conflict-of-interest questions, particularly since the county commission oversees some PUD-related land use and regulatory matters.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Hadaller can argue his PUD experience gives him relevant governing experience and that he would resign the PUD seat if elected to the county commission. Many candidates have transitioned from one elected office to another.

Finding 6.3: East County Base — Geographic Advantage Over Incumbent MODERATE

What happened
Hadaller is a lifetime East Lewis County resident (Morton area), giving him a natural base in the rural eastern portion of District 3 that has long felt underrepresented. This contrasts with incumbent Brummer, who lives in Winlock — the western edge of the district, 70+ miles from Packwood. The east county representation gap is a central issue in the 2026 race and the "Lewis County 3 to 5" expansion campaign.
