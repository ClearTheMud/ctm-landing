1 Financial Conflicts & Industry Alignment

Finding 1.1: Conflict of Interest — Property Manager Voting on Housing Policy CRITICAL

What happened
Barkis owns Hometown Property Management, managing approximately 1,200 rental units in Thurston County. He simultaneously serves as Ranking Minority Member on the Housing committee, is the legislature’s most vocal opponent of rent control, and sponsors landlord-tenant reform legislation. His NARPM certifications (CRMC, MPM, RMP) demonstrate deep industry alignment. He has spoken directly to NARPM members about landlord concerns during COVID. His business directly benefits from policies he advocates for — opposing rent caps means higher potential revenues on 1,200 units; increasing housing supply means more units to manage.
Source tier
T2 (Multi-Source Media)
Defense
Barkis has disclosed his business interests and argues his professional experience makes him uniquely qualified to shape housing policy. No formal ethics complaint has been filed.
Political impact
This is the single most exploitable vulnerability in a general election: a landlord managing 1,200 units who chairs the Housing committee and blocks rent control. Attack framing writes itself.

Finding 1.2: Endorsed Exclusively by Landlord/Developer Interests HIGH

What happened
All endorsements come from real estate, construction, and landlord organizations: BIAW, NAIOP, BOMA, WA Multi-Family Housing Association, Affordable Housing Council. No labor, environmental, or progressive group endorsements. Top donors include WA Multi-Family Housing PAC at $2,400. This concentration raises questions about whose interests he represents on the Housing committee.
Source tier
T1 (WA PDC contribution records, campaign endorsement page)
Defense
Barkis points to his lot-splitting bill (HB 1096, signed into law) as evidence he works to increase housing supply for all.
Political impact
An opponent could construct a “bought by the industry he regulates” narrative using exclusively public-record data. The absence of any non-industry endorsements amplifies the pattern.

2 Voting Record Vulnerabilities

Finding 2.1: Voted Against Reproductive Rights Legislation MODERATE

What happened
Barkis voted against HB 1851 (expanding abortion provider access) and HB 1009 (student health plan abortion coverage). In a state where reproductive rights have strong majority support, these votes create vulnerability in a general election context.
Source tier
T1 (WA Legislature official roll call records)
Defense
LD-2 leans 60–65% Republican; these votes align with the district’s conservative base. Barkis has not been publicly outspoken on abortion.
Political impact
In a general election, these votes could be used to mobilize suburban women and younger voters who favor reproductive access. In a primary, they are assets.

3 Electoral Accountability

Finding 3.1: No Competitive Race Since 2018 — Effectively Unaccountable MODERATE

What happened
Barkis has run unopposed (write-in only) in 3 of his last 5 general elections (2020, 2022, 2024), winning with 93–96% margins. He has not faced a serious challenger since 2018. This lack of electoral competition means he has not been forced to publicly defend his policy positions or business interests to voters.
Source tier
T1 (WA SOS certified election results, Ballotpedia)
Defense
His consistently high margins demonstrate broad district support. Running unopposed is common in safe districts.
Political impact
Seven years without competitive scrutiny means Barkis has never had to answer for his conflict-of-interest profile in a campaign context. A well-funded challenger could exploit this narrative: “Who is Andrew Barkis actually accountable to?”
