Finding 4.1: Prosecutor filed and a Superior Court judge upheld affidavits of prejudice barring Rands from 123 DUI cases in his first month on the bench MODERATE

What happened
Within days of Rands taking office in January 2023, the Whatcom County Prosecuting Attorney's Office filed affidavits of prejudice on 123 DUI cases, seeking to keep the newly elected judge from hearing them. Prosecutor Eric Richey cited Rands's prior career as a DUI defense attorney and a concern that Rands would rule on dismissal motions built on arguments Rands had developed as defense counsel, motions other district court judges had already rejected. Superior Court Judge Robert Olsen upheld the effort in February 2023. An affidavit of prejudice is a routine one-time disqualification tool, but filing 123 at once before a judge has ruled on any case is unusual. Richey stated the filings covered about six percent of the district court's caseload.

Defense
Rands's counsel called the timing unprecedented and noted the state had multiple earlier windows to act (after the November 2022 election, after certification, and after his caseload assignment became public) rather than immediately after he was sworn in. The affidavits addressed a defined set of DUI cases and did not allege misconduct. Voters elected Rands with 62 percent in 2022 with full knowledge of his DUI-defense background, and in 2022 he was endorsed across party lines and by sitting Superior Court judges.
