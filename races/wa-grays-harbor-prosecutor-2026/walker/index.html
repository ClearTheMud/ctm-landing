Statement

My proven experience at the Grays Harbor Prosecutor’s Office and the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys (WAPA) makes me uniquely qualified to lead the office.

I served as a Grays Harbor Deputy Prosecutor and Chief Criminal Deputy for 11 years, prosecuting serious crimes including drug dealing, rape, and murder.  I've tried more than 85 cases to a jury.  I convicted the woman who stabbed an Aberdeen hotel owner to death, and convicted the man responsible for the 2003 McCleary cold case rape.  I'm also an appellate attorney, having defended more than 60 convictions on appeal.

At WAPA, I worked on recruiting and retention in rural counties, created the first statewide boot camp for new prosecutors, and worked with nearly every county prosecutor in Washington.  I know what works in strong and effective prosecutor’s offices.

During my time away from the Grays Harbor Prosecutor's office, turnover increased dramatically.  With too little training and the loss of institutional knowledge, provable charges were reduced or dismissed.  I will fix this.  I have the experience in this office and the statewide perspective to rebuild stability, strengthen training, support victims and law enforcement, and ensure serious criminals are held accountable.  I ask for your vote.

Finding 6.1: Appointed prosecutor through a party process, never elected MODERATE

What happened
Walker was appointed prosecutor on March 17, 2026 through a partisan nomination process. The Grays Harbor Democrats submitted three nominees (Walker, Brian Lane, Mac Jardine), and the BOCC selected Walker. He has not won a competitive election for the office.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Washington law requires partisan appointment when a partisan office is vacated mid-term. The process is public and legally required, and Walker's selection cited 11 years in the office plus statewide training leadership.
