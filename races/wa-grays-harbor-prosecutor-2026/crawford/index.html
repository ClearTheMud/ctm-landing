Finding 5.1: Challenging His Own Boss — Subordinate vs Appointed Superior HIGH

What happened
Crawford serves as Chief Criminal Deputy Prosecutor under Walker, who was appointed over the position Crawford is now running for. The BOCC chose Walker through the Democratic Party nomination process rather than elevating Crawford, the existing #2 in the office. This creates an unusual dynamic where the challenger reports directly to the incumbent daily.

T2
Political impact
High
Defense
Crawford was not eligible for appointment through the Democratic process because he is a Republican. The appointment followed partisan rules, not merit selection. Crawford's decision to run demonstrates independence and willingness to let voters decide rather than accept a party-line appointment.

Crawford has been in Grays Harbor County only since August 2024 (~22 months). He came from Pacific County before that and Grant County before that. Walker, by contrast, spent 11 years in the Grays Harbor office.

Finding 5.3: Compensation Clash with County Commissioners MODERATE

What happened
Crawford publicly clashed with Commissioner Georgia Miller over prosecutor salary negotiations in late 2025. Miller suggested 'workplace culture and leadership issues — not compensation — were causing retention problems' and cited internal investigations and pending litigation from former employees. Crawford's salary advocacy was effective but the public disagreement with a commissioner could be a liability.
