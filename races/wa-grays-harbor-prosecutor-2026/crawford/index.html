1 Voters’ Pamphlet Statement

Elected Experience

None

Other Professional Experience

USMC veteran and career prosecutor with more than 15 years of public service. I have tried more than 100 jury trials and dedicated my career to protecting victims, supporting law enforcement, and seeking justice with integrity, transparency, and accountability for community safety.  


Education

First generation college graduate. Graduated from South Kitsap High School in Washington in 1990. BA from USC. JD from Syracuse Law.


Community Service

Through my business, I support local recovery programs by offering free physical training to build strength, connection, accountability, and hope. It is an honor to stand with families and friends affected by a loved one’s addiction.

Statement

Grays Harbor is not just where I work; it is where my family and I are deeply rooted in the community. As a husband, homeowner, business owner, CrossFit coach, and public servant, I care deeply about the future of the place we call home and the people who live here.

For more than 15 years, I have dedicated my career to protecting victims, pursuing justice, and standing up for public safety with integrity and transparency. I understand how crime impacts families, businesses, neighborhoods, and the sense of security every community deserves.

As a USMC veteran and career prosecutor, I believe accountability, structure, compassion, and support must work together to create lasting community change. I also believe leadership means setting the example through humility, transparency, and service; especially when the work becomes difficult.

Our community deserves a Prosecutor’s Office focused on integrity, accountability, and protecting the vulnerable; not politics as usual. I will work to build trust, support victims and families, and strengthen public safety while treating people fairly and respectfully. We have a duty to ensure victims have a voice. Together, we can build a safer and stronger future for Grays Harbor.

Finding 6.1: Runs against the appointed incumbent who is his current supervisor LOW

What happened
Crawford is Chief Criminal Deputy Prosecutor under Walker, who was appointed to the prosecutor position through the county Democratic Party nomination process. Crawford, a Republican, was not eligible for that partisan appointment and is now running for the office while reporting to Walker.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Low
Defense
Crawford was not eligible for the appointment because it followed partisan rules rather than merit selection. Running lets voters decide the office rather than settle it by appointment.

Finding 6.2: Less than two years in Grays Harbor County MODERATE

What happened
Crawford has worked in Grays Harbor County since August 2024, about 22 months. He came from Pacific County before that and Grant County before that. Walker spent 11 years in the Grays Harbor office.
Source tier
T2
Political impact

Defense
Crawford was recruited to Grays Harbor at a higher salary for his trial skills and has already won major convictions in the county (Carter, Palmer, Baier cases). His Pacific County prosecutor called him "the backbone of the criminal division."

Finding 6.3: Public dispute with a commissioner over prosecutor pay MODERATE

What happened
Crawford publicly clashed with Commissioner Georgia Miller over prosecutor salary negotiations in late 2025. Miller said workplace culture and leadership issues, not compensation, were causing retention problems, and cited internal investigations and pending litigation from former employees.

T2

Defense
