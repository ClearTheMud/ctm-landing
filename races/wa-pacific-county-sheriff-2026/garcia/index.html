Finding 6.1: Took office with no law enforcement experience; a 2026 state law was amended to let him keep the job MODERATE

What happened
Garcia was elected Pacific County Sheriff in 2022 with no prior law enforcement experience and completed the state Basic Law Enforcement Academy during his first year in office. In 2026 the Legislature passed Senate Bill 5974, which set new experience requirements for sheriffs and police chiefs. As originally drafted the bill would have required five years of uninterrupted law enforcement experience, a standard Garcia did not meet and that would have forced him from office. The final version, passed early on March 6, 2026, exempted sitting sheriffs and police chiefs, allowing Garcia to remain in office and run for reelection.

Defense
Garcia completed the mandated Basic Law Enforcement Academy within his first year as required by law and has served the full term. The final statute exempts all sitting sheriffs, not Garcia alone, and reflects a general policy choice by the Legislature rather than a personal qualification granted to him.
