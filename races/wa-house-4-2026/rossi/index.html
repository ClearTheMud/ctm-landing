Statement
Our Constitutional Republic is facing an unsustainable fiscal reckoning. With the national debt at 100% of GDP and projected to be greater than 200% within 30 years, interest alone will soon consume the majority of our tax revenue. Economists warn our fiscal trajectory is incompatible with a functioning long-term economy.  Every year of delay adds trillions to the cost of reform as four million more baby boomers retire. There is no painless solution, but comprehensive bipartisan reform is necessary to restore fiscal stability.  How can we fix it?

Squeeze Health Inefficiencies: Transition Medicare to a premium support system where private insurers compete for retirees. This empowers seniors with choices while potentially lowering federal costs by 8%. Implementing a per-capita cap on Medicaid can help stop the cycle of endless federal subsidies while incentivizing state-level innovation.

Modernize Entitlements: Existing entitlement programs cannot be sustained under today’s demographic realities. Raising the age of eligibility and switching to price-indexing for initial benefits can help. The program must focus on protecting the vulnerable with a 125% poverty-line minimum benefit, while asking the top 60% of earners to cover more of their Medicare premiums.

Restrain General Spending: Cap all discretionary spending growth at 3.5% annually. This brings defense and domestic outlays to their lowest share of the economy since the 1930s while ending wasteful farm and student loan subsidies.

Realistic Tax Reform: We must end the fantasy that taxing billionaires solves this—seizing their total wealth would only fund the government for nine months. Raising the top tax bracket to 39.6%, capping deductions at 15%, and implementing a 1% Medicare payroll tax hike could help ensure the system’s survival. 

Politicians of both parties created this crisis by overpromising. This is a call to make them responsible for fixing it.
