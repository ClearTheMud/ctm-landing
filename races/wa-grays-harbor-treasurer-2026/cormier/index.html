Finding 4.2: No Operational Treasurer Experience — Executive vs Operational Gap MODERATE

What happened
Cormier's experience is executive/legislative (budget oversight, policy decisions as commissioner) and appraisal-based (real estate appraiser in Assessor's Office). He has no direct experience in treasury operations — deposit management, property tax processing, investment of county funds, or the day-to-day accounting that the treasurer's office performs. His opponent has 9 years of hands-on operational experience in the very office.

Defense
Cormier oversaw a $100+ million county budget for 8 years, making financial policy decisions and managing contracts. His 7 years in the Assessor's Office gave him direct experience with property tax and levy systems. Executive oversight experience is arguably more important than operational experience for a department head role.

Finding 4.3: Weyerhaeuser Ordinance Repeal — Controversial Policy Record MODERATE

What happened
As commissioner, Cormier championed an ordinance targeting Weyerhaeuser and large timber companies that charged recreational access fees on private land receiving property tax breaks. The timber industry fought back, and the commissioners ultimately repealed the ordinance. In a timber-dependent county, taking on major employers is a double-edged sword.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Cormier argued for taxpayer equity — public access was part of the designated forest land tax break. His willingness to challenge powerful interests demonstrates independence. He expressed disappointment when the ordinance was repealed, showing conviction.
