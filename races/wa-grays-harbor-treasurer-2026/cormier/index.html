Statement

As a former two term Grays Harbor County Commissioner, Wes Cormier helped oversee county operations, set policy, and pass balanced budgets that served the residents of Grays Harbor. During his time in office, he worked directly on financial oversight, budget planning, and responsible stewardship of your public dollars.

Cormier’s years of experience includes proposing, reviewing and approving multi-million dollar county budgets, evaluating department funding requests, overseeing public expenditures, and helping to ensure the county remained financially accountable to the public. During his eight years, he worked closely with elected officials, department heads, and staff to maintain essential public services while emphasizing fiscal responsibility and transparency.

Before serving as County Commissioner, Cormier worked as a Senior Real Estate Appraiser, giving him a depth of knowledge of property valuation and the levy system.  Experience that is relevant to the position of County Treasurer.

During his time as Commissioner, Cormier was always a pragmatic decision maker with a focus on working families, local businesses, and the community. If elected, he will continue emphasizing accountability, integrity, and protecting the interests of Grays Harbor residents.

Finding 5.2: No direct treasury-operations experience MODERATE

What happened
Cormier's experience is executive and legislative (budget oversight and policy as a commissioner) and appraisal-based (real estate appraiser in the Assessor's Office). He has no direct experience in treasury operations such as deposit management, property tax processing, investment of county funds, or day-to-day accounting. His opponent has nine years of operational experience in the office.
Source tier
T2
Political impact

Defense
Cormier oversaw a county budget above $100 million for eight years and made financial policy decisions, and his seven years in the Assessor's Office gave him direct experience with property tax and levy systems.

Finding 5.3: Championed a timber-access ordinance that was later repealed MODERATE

What happened
As commissioner, Cormier championed an ordinance targeting Weyerhaeuser and large timber companies that charged recreational access fees on private land receiving property tax breaks. The timber industry pushed back, and the commissioners later repealed the ordinance.
Source tier
T2
Political impact

Defense
Cormier argued for taxpayer equity, since public access was tied to the designated forest land tax break, and said he was disappointed when the ordinance was repealed.
