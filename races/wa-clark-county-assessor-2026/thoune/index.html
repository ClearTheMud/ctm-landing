Statement

Clark County homeowners deserve assessments that reflect the real market — not a system that misses the mark. When ordinary homeowners’ values rise while multimillion-dollar properties remain dramatically under-assessed, it raises serious questions about accuracy and fairness.

One luxury Clark County home sold December 22, 2023 for $13.5 million — just 10 days before the January 1, 2024 valuation date — but was assessed at only $5,955,707, about 44% of the sale price. Based on local levy rates, that difference represents roughly $75,000 per year less in property taxes than if the home had been assessed at its sale price. When high-value properties are under-assessed, the tax burden can shift to other taxpayers — like you. 

The Board of Equalization is an independent county board where taxpayers appeal their property valuations. In 2024, 56% of valuation cases that reached the Board were reduced or overturned, raising serious concerns about whether many residents were over-assessed in the first place. 

As Assessor, Tyler Thoune will fight for fair valuations, transparency, accountability for mistakes, better customer service, and common-sense assessment practices grounded in the actual Clark County market. Tyler will make sure your property is treated fairly so you don’t overpay.
