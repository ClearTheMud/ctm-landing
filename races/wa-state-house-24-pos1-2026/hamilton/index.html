Statement
I know what it means to grow up in a community where the working class is asked to do more with less. Students are still recovering from the pandemic learning loss, and families are struggling to make ends meet. I lived through that crisis as a student and saw the cost firsthand: lost instruction time, parents shouldering the burden, and workers left behind. 

While Washington is in an affordability crisis, my opponent voted to raise your cost of living. We are paying more, getting less, and watching Olympia celebrate failure rather than fight for us. We can do better.

2 Political Positions

TopicPositionSource
TaxesWants to make it harder to pass new taxes, requiring both parties to work together before increasing the financial burden on residents. Supports repealing Washington's new tax on income over $1 million, arguing new taxes act as a catalyst for more spending.T2
State BudgetContends the state budget grew from about $30 billion to $80 billion without proportional benefit to residents, and attributes the current deficit to overspending against a revenue growth cap. Advocates a spending freeze while preserving existing programs.T2
HousingWants to make housing more affordable without placing extra burdens on landlords. Proposes reviewing environmental regulations for cost-effectiveness, noting such rules can add substantially to construction costs, and emphasizes traditional single-family housing.T2
EducationCampaigns to fix educational outcomes and restore education in Washington, drawing on his own pandemic learning-loss experience. Supports school choice as a way to address learning loss.T2
