Statement

It has been an honor to serve as your Sheriff over the past four years. The Sheriff’s Office has made significant progress in keeping our community safe. We increased staffing for patrol, investigations, and support services, which help reduce response times. The Body Worn Camera program was introduced to enhance transparency and accountability while protecting deputies. Investments in tools such as the Grappler have improved our ability to safely apprehend offenders and protect victims. In collaboration with other regional law enforcement partners, we opened the SW Washington Police Academy to raise training standards and efficiency. CCSO also expanded community engagement through public forums, neighborhood outreach, communications, and social media.

Even with these improvements, the Sheriff’s Office remains among the lowest-funded agencies in the state based on the officer-to-citizen ratio. Increasing CCSO staffing will remain my top priority. Additional deputies will enable dedicated positions for traffic enforcement, retail theft, major crimes, child victim support, drug investigations, warrants, civil, and evidence units.    

I ask for your vote to build a safer, stronger community.

Finding 6.1: Documented 2004 demotion and suspension in his sheriff's office personnel record MODERATE

What happened
Horch's Clark County Sheriff's Office personnel file records formal discipline in 2004. In May 2004 he was involuntarily demoted from Enforcement Sergeant to Deputy Sheriff II following a domestic-incident report involving his then-wife that occurred during duty hours in April 2004. Later that year he received a two-week suspension without pay for removing diversity posters from two precincts, which he said he did while upset over the demotion. A 2000 evaluation also noted he mislaid a firearm booked into property until it was found in his patrol car, with no formal discipline. The record was reviewed and reported during his 2022 campaign, and Horch has publicly acknowledged it.
Source tier

Defense
The discipline is more than 20 years old and predates any command or elected role. Horch has publicly acknowledged it, attributed it to a period of personal difficulty around his divorce, and stated he stopped drinking in 2004 and joined a recovery group he continues to attend. He was subsequently promoted through the ranks to Chief Criminal Deputy and elected sheriff in 2022. Voters returned him to a supervisory career and then to the top office after this record was public.
