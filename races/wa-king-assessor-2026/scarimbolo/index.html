1 Voters’ Pamphlet Statement (General Election 2026)

Education: B.A. Criminal Justice, Washington State University

Occupation: Small Family Business Owner, CAPR King County Chapter President

Statement: The King County Assessor is far more than an appraiser—it’s absolutely a leadership role, of one of the largest public offices affecting every property owner. While the office does employ appraisal staff, the Assessor's responsibility ensures the entire system operates transparently, consistently, fairly, and in accordance with the law.

I have studied our property tax system from the taxpayers perspective, analyzing assessment data, researching mass appraisal practices, public records requests, appealing assessments, reviewing BoE decisions, and developed comprehensive audit proposals identifying significant areas for reform. That experience gives me something many candidates cannot claim: a taxpayer's perspective on how the system actually functions in practice.

My campaign isn’t about making minor adjustments to the status quo. It’s about pursuing meaningful, system-wide reforms that protect taxpayers, strengthen property rights, ensures due process and helps ensure people aren’t taxed out of their homes.

Government should be judged by results—not intentions. Our current assessment system is required to produce fair and uniform assessments, but if that system does not produce the results required by law, we have a responsibility to examine it honestly, measure its performance, and improve it.

Property owners deserve information they can understand. They deserve parcel-specific breakdowns on their individual property valuations—not simply a final number. They deserve due process, timely appeals and logical evidentiary standards, and should never need to become valuation experts or pay for professional help, simply to challenge their assessment.

I’m a King County resident, small business owner, and President of the King County CAPR chapter. I’m not a politician. Government works best being fully transparent and accountable, and we demand fiscal responsibility. I will continue to work relentlessly to change the status quo, strengthen taxpayer protections, and advocate for significant reforms that restore fairness and accountability in one of King County’s most important public offices.

Finding 5.1: Leads an advocacy group that publicly contests the tax system she seeks to administer LOW

What happened
Scarimbolo is King County Chapter President of the Citizens' Alliance for Property Rights (CAPR), an advocacy organization that campaigns against Washington's property tax structure and argues the mass-appraisal system produces unlawful results. She carries that position directly into her candidacy, stating in her voters' pamphlet that current assessments are highly non-uniform in a way she contends violates state law. A voter can reasonably weigh whether a candidate who leads an organized opposition to the current property tax system would administer the neutral valuation office impartially.

Defense
The assessor values property uniformly and does not set tax rates or levy amounts, which are fixed by voters and taxing districts. Advocacy for taxpayer transparency and accurate, uniform assessments is consistent with the assessor's legal duty, and firsthand appeal experience is a qualification many candidates lack. Holding a policy viewpoint is not a legal conflict of interest.
