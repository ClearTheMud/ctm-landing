1 Voters’ Pamphlet Statement

Education: B.A. Criminal Justice, Washington State University

Occupation: Small Business Owner, CAPR King County Chapter President

Statement: The growing property tax burden in King County is unsustainable for many residents. Families, retirees, and small business owners should not feel like they are being taxed out of their homes or land because assessments continue to rise faster than ordinary people can keep up. We need property tax reform, and we need it now, and that starts with admitting our current system does not produce uniform or equitable results as intended.  In a system legally and constitutionally required to have uniformity, results are ultimately what matter.

Our current property tax system is both too difficult to understand and challenge.  Based on my independent research, the system produces highly non-uniform results, violating both state law and our WA state constitution.

I am proudly not a politician.  I am a 35+ year King County resident, small family business owner, and King Countys’ Citizens Alliance for Property Rights (CAPR) Chapter President.  I have personally gone through the property tax appeal process, so I understand how bad and frustrating our current system is.

I’ve reviewed assessment data, submitted public records requests, filed appeals, researched appraisal data, and tried to work within the system, to challenge it and better understand it. Our system completely lacks transparency, consistency, accountability, and any kind of meaningful due process for taxpayers across the board.

I have recently developed and submitted multiple audit proposals targeting our current property taxation system.  Property owners deserve a system where assessments are easily understandable, valuation methods are transparent, standards are applied fairly and uniformly, and taxpayer rights are respected throughout the appeals process.

Regardless of being elected as Assessor, I will continue to work on property tax reform. Given the significant tax burden property owners carry, we deserve a system we have confidence in and that doesn’t tax us out of our homes.

Finding 5.1: Leads an advocacy group that publicly contests the tax system she seeks to administer LOW

What happened
Scarimbolo is King County Chapter President of the Citizens' Alliance for Property Rights (CAPR), an advocacy organization that campaigns against Washington's property tax structure and argues the mass-appraisal system produces unlawful results. She carries that position directly into her candidacy, stating in her voters' pamphlet that current assessments are highly non-uniform in a way she contends violates state law. A voter can reasonably weigh whether a candidate who leads an organized opposition to the current property tax system would administer the neutral valuation office impartially.

Defense
The assessor values property uniformly and does not set tax rates or levy amounts, which are fixed by voters and taxing districts. Advocacy for taxpayer transparency and accurate, uniform assessments is consistent with the assessor's legal duty, and firsthand appeal experience is a qualification many candidates lack. Holding a policy viewpoint is not a legal conflict of interest.
