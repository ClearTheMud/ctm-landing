Finding 6.1: Sheriff's office deployed Flock license plate cameras now subject to a pending public-records lawsuit MODERATE

What happened
Under Sheriff Scheyer, the Skamania County Sheriff's Office deployed a network of six Flock Safety automated license plate reader cameras under a two-year contract of about $39,000 running through December 2026. An Oregon resident, Jose Rodriguez, filed a public-records lawsuit challenging the Skamania system, which remained pending as of November 2025. After a November 6, 2025 Skagit County Superior Court ruling found similar Flock footage to be a public record subject to disclosure, Scheyer disabled all six cameras on November 12, 2025, while the physical infrastructure remained in place.

Defense
Scheyer disabled the cameras in November 2025 and publicly raised privacy concerns, saying somebody may have nefarious intentions to gain the information, such as an estranged spouse stalking their ex. She described the devices as license plate readers that do not photograph drivers and said the record is not held by the agency unless the information is downloaded. She also said the county could not exit the contract before it ends in December 2026 and that she does not intend to renew it.
