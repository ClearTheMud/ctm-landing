Finding 7.1: Concealed a workplace relationship that triggered a county investigation and a new disclosure policy MODERATE

What happened
As Chief Criminal Deputy Prosecutor, Sigmar kept a romantic relationship with a legal assistant in the office secret for nearly a year. An anonymous complaint in December 2024 alleged favoritism and mismanagement, and the county commissioned a third-party investigation by the Bothell law firm Haggard and Ganson. The investigation found no policy violation by Sigmar (the county had no disclosure requirement at the time) and did not substantiate the favoritism allegations, noting the employee received no unfair promotion, pay raise, or benefit, and Prosecutor Eric Richey took no disciplinary action. Even so, the episode fueled allegations of a divided, low-morale office, at least one deputy prosecutor resigned citing the need for transparency, and the county afterward expanded its policy to require disclosure of romantic relationships that create potential conflicts. Sigmar, a supervisor in the office at the time, has said he should have disclosed the relationship earlier.

Defense
The independent investigation found no policy violation and did not substantiate favoritism, and no discipline was imposed. Sigmar has said he supports the disclosure policy the county adopted and acknowledges he could have avoided the perception of wrongdoing by disclosing the relationship sooner.
