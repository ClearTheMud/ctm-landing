Finding 7.1: Removed from a California court commissioner post over jailing of suspects MODERATE

What happened
Earlier in her career, Bracke served as a court commissioner at the Glendale Municipal Court in California in the mid-1990s. Cascadia Daily News reported in its July 2026 candidate profile, and Bracke acknowledges, that she was fired after being accused of wrongly jailing people suspected of perjury for providing false proof of insurance. Bracke attributes the outcome to a corrupt judge who was later censured and barred from future judgeships. Public records confirm that part of her account. The California Commission on Judicial Performance publicly censured Judge James R. Simpson of the Glendale Municipal Court and barred him from future assignments on December 9, 2002, and its record documents that Simpson repeatedly contacted Commissioner Bracke between 1995 and 1997 to try to influence traffic cases involving his friends. Those records concern Simpson's conduct rather than Bracke's removal, and the specific reason for her firing rests on the Cascadia account and Bracke's own words. California and Washington bar records show no attorney discipline against her.

Defense
Bracke says she was the target of a corrupt judge who was later censured, a judge that the California Commission on Judicial Performance did censure and bar from future assignments in 2002, and that the removal was from a judicial-officer appointment rather than her law license. She has no bar discipline in either California or Washington, and the judicial-performance record indicates she declined the special treatment the judge sought for his friends. She points to roughly 45 years of prosecution, defense, civil, and bench experience and more than 40 jury trials.
