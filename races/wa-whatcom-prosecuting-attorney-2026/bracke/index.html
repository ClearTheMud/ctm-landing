Statement
Keeping Whatcom County safe, reducing property crimes, and protecting victims and the vulnerable requires proven experience.  I’ll bring unmatched expertise, building and implementing effective crime-reduction strategies, diversion programs, and victim support, particularly for victims of domestic violence and sexual assault.

It’s time for a change! As the most experienced candidate, my plan is to set the office on a new course, prioritizing justice while respecting victims. I am ready to eliminate the backlog, restore office stability, support employees, strengthen trust, and lead with experience and respect.

I know what it means to have integrity. Early in my career, I was fired as a Court Commissioner by the presiding judge for refusing to “fix” traffic tickets for the judges’ friends. The Commission on Judicial Performance eventually charged him, he admitted the allegations, and retired when removed from the bench.

Starting as a young prosecutor, I was a leader in adopting intervention programs. I believe in treatment over incarceration for lower-level crimes, addiction disorders, and mental health conditions. I am also a supporter of specialty courts such as Mental Health Court, Recovery Court, and Veterans Court. With your vote, I will lead with fairness and be dedicated to public safety.

Finding 7.1: Removed from a California court commissioner post over jailing of suspects MODERATE

What happened
Earlier in her career, while serving as a court commissioner in Glendale, California, Bracke was fired after being accused of wrongly jailing people suspected of perjury for providing false proof of insurance. The account was reported by Cascadia Daily News in its July 2026 candidate profile and is acknowledged by Bracke herself. She attributes the outcome to a corrupt judge who she says was later censured and barred from future judgeships. California and Washington bar records show no attorney discipline against her.

Defense
Bracke says she was the target of a corrupt judge who was later censured, that the removal was from a judicial-officer appointment rather than her law license, and that she has no bar discipline in either California or Washington. She points to roughly 45 years of prosecution, defense, civil, and bench experience and more than 40 jury trials.
