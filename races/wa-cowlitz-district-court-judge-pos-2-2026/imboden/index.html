Finding 3.1: Censured by the state Commission on Judicial Conduct after a 2020 impaired-driving arrest HIGH

What happened
The Washington State Commission on Judicial Conduct entered a Stipulation, Agreement and Order of Censure for Cowlitz County District Court Judge Michael J. Imboden (M. Jamie Imboden) on April 22, 2022, in case CJC No. 9906-F-197. Under the stipulated facts, Imboden was arrested in Kelso on April 17, 2020 for driving under the influence of alcohol and/or drugs. The arresting officers reported that he referenced his judicial position during the encounter, which Imboden disputes, and he acknowledged asking the officers if they knew what an arrest would do to his career. Because Imboden is a Cowlitz County judge, the criminal case was transferred to Clark County to avoid conflicts of interest. He pleaded guilty to an amended charge of reckless driving on November 19, 2021, and a Clark County District Court judge imposed a sentence of community work service, a fine and court costs in cause No. XZ0358728, which the stipulation records he promptly satisfied. Imboden agreed that he violated Canon 1, Rules 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct. The Commission censured him and ordered him to read the Code and to speak at no fewer than three public appearances on matters related to his stipulated misconduct. On November 21, 2025 the Commission issued a Certificate of Completion and Order finding that he had satisfactorily completed the terms and conditions of the censure. Censure is a public disciplinary sanction by the body that oversees judicial conduct in Washington.

Defense
Imboden entered a stipulated agreement rather than contesting the matter, contacted the Commission on the Monday following his arrest, and remained cooperative throughout the proceeding. He maintains he did not seek preferential treatment from the arresting officers and that none was given. The Commission recorded that this was an isolated incident, that he had no prior judicial misconduct history, that the conduct occurred outside the courtroom, and that no recommendation to the state supreme court for suspension or removal was warranted. The Commission certified on November 21, 2025 that he had satisfactorily completed all terms and conditions. He now serves as Presiding Judge of the Cowlitz County District Court.
