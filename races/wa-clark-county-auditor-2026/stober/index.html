Statement

Clark County Auditor is one of the county’s most important nonpartisan offices. The Auditor oversees elections, public records, licensing services, and financial systems that residents rely on every day. This job requires more than good intentions. It requires management experience, financial oversight expertise, attention to detail, and the ability to lead professional teams responsibly.

I’ve built those qualifications through both public service and private-sector leadership. For eleven years on Vancouver City Council, I’ve helped oversee large public budgets, complex government systems, and long-term organizational planning. In the private sector, I led teams overseeing finance, operations, and technology, with responsibility for budgets, systems implementation, and team performance.

I’ve also studied election administration as a Certified Elections Observer because public trust in elections matters. The Clark County Auditor’s Office has earned a strong reputation for professionalism, accuracy, and integrity. I will work every day to protect and strengthen that reputation by supporting professional staff, maintaining secure and transparent elections, safeguarding public records, and ensuring taxpayer resources are managed responsibly.

This office should be calm, competent, nonpartisan, and focused on getting the details right. I would be honored to earn your vote.

Finding 5.1: PDC issued a written warning to Stober's 2023 council campaign for accepting an over-limit contribution LOW

What happened
On August 2, 2023, the Washington Public Disclosure Commission opened enforcement case 140460 after a complaint that Ty Stober's Vancouver City Council campaign received and deposited a contribution above the statutory limit set by RCW 42.17A.405. The over-limit portion traced to a donor whose original 2021 gift was sized on the expectation that additional opponents would appear on the general election ballot. After the complaint, the campaign refunded the over-limit amount and amended the affected reports. PDC staff treated it as a minor violation and closed the case with a written warning, with no monetary penalty, noting the expectation that Stober would follow contribution limits in future campaigns.

Defense
The matter was resolved as a minor violation with a written warning and no fine. The campaign refunded the over-limit contribution and corrected its reports once the issue was raised, and the original contribution reflected a good-faith reading of how many candidates would reach the general election ballot. The complaint was filed by Glen Morgan, who has filed large volumes of PDC complaints across Washington campaigns.
