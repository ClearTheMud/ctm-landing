I am proud to represent Washington’s 7th district and stand up for working people every single day. As your Congresswoman, I've worked to protect our democracy, raise wages, lower costs, build affordable housing, protect abortion rights, and fight climate change.   

I’m leading the fight for the Medicare for All Act to ensure that universal healthcare is a right, not a privilege. I also lead landmark legislation to make higher education tuition-free, guarantee housing as a human right, expand workers’ rights, raise the federal minimum wage, implement humane immigration reform, and establish an ultra-millionaire wealth tax. I lead bipartisan bills that ban stock trading for Members of Congress and protect Americans from government spying. I’m doing everything possible to get money out of politics and reform the Supreme Court.

I’ve been on the frontlines of holding Trump accountable, including around the lawless ICE and CBP actions that have kidnapped and disappeared our neighbors. I established the Monopoly Busters Caucus to fight consolidation, corporate greed, and price-gouging. I have authored a War Powers Resolution to end Trump’s illegal war in Iran and worked with Epstein survivors to get justice against predators and pedophiles.

I’m proud to represent a diverse, innovative and compassionate district. I have never taken corporate PAC money because I fight for working people, not the billionaires and giant corporations. I would be deeply honored to have your vote.

Finding 6.1: Late STOCK Act Filing While Leading the Push to Ban Congressional Stock Trading MODERATE

What happened
Jayapal filed a periodic transaction report in March 2026 for a stock sale she made in 2023, missing the 45-day disclosure deadline required by the STOCK Act. The transaction involved shares of Newell Brands, valued between $1,001 and $15,000, that she had received as a gift from a family member more than a decade earlier and held as the only individual stock in her portfolio. She had reported the sale on her annual 2023 financial disclosure but did not file the separate periodic transaction report until the House Ethics Committee flagged the discrepancy. The lapse is notable because Jayapal is one of the House's most prominent advocates for barring members of Congress from owning and trading individual stocks and has repeatedly introduced legislation to that effect.
Source tier

Political impact

Defense
Jayapal disclosed the sale on her annual financial disclosure, held only one gifted stock, and attributed the missed periodic report to an oversight rather than concealment. The standard penalty for such a filing lapse is a $200 fine. NOTUS reported that she filed the report promptly once the Ethics Committee raised it.
