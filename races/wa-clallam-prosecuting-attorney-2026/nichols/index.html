Finding 4.1: Federal sexual harassment lawsuit against Nichols settled for $350,000 in 2018 HIGH

What happened
Tina Hendrickson, the office manager of the Clallam County Prosecuting Attorney's Office, sued Mark Nichols in federal court on June 1, 2017, alleging he subjected her to sexual harassment between April 2015 and April 2017 in violation of the Equal Protection Clause and 42 U.S.C. section 1983 (Hendrickson v. Nichols, No. 3:17-cv-05418-BHS, U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington at Tacoma). Nichols was named in his individual capacity and was the only defendant; Clallam County was not a party. These are allegations in a pleading that was never tried. Nichols denied the allegations. He moved for summary judgment in September 2018 and Judge Benjamin Settle denied the motion, allowing the case to proceed toward a jury trial. A denial of summary judgment is a procedural ruling and decides nothing on the merits. The case never reached a verdict. The parties settled, a stipulation was filed December 24, 2018, and the court terminated the case December 26, 2018. The settlement agreement was never filed on the docket, so its terms do not appear in the court record. The Peninsula Daily News, in reporting published December 13, 2018, said its reporter reviewed the signed agreement and that the settlement paid Hendrickson $350,000, with the payment coming from the statewide insurance risk pool the county belongs to rather than from Nichols personally. That dollar figure rests on that reporting, not on the court record. The Peninsula Daily News quoted the agreement as stating that it "shall not constitute, in any manner, an admission by Nichols that he has violated any law or failed to fulfill any duty to Hendrickson or anyone else."

Defense
No court ever ruled on the merits of the allegations, and the settlement contains an express disclaimer of any admission of wrongdoing by Nichols. Nichols denied the allegations throughout and said he was prepared to go to trial. In a statement issued after the settlement, Nichols said the outcome was consistent with the findings of an independent investigator retained by the county. That report has not been released publicly, the county asserted attorney-client privilege over it, and Hendrickson's attorney publicly disputed Nichols' characterization of it. Voters returned Nichols to office in November 2018, weeks before the settlement, and again in 2022.
