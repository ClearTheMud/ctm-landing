Statement

Eastern Washington deserves better than the lowest-common-denominator candidates our two parties continue to offer. My political experience spans over 30 years, beginning at junior college as an intern for Congresswoman Barbara Boxer. After retiring from the military, I managed three congressional campaigns, including here in the 5th District. I lobbied for my union in California’s legislature and remain politically active. I’ve learned the system – but it’s failing.

Anyone who has seen me on the PBS NewsHour or other news outlets knows that I was not looking to run for congress: I was asked by supporters.

Voters are frustrated with politicians who prioritize political advancement over the needs of working families. Veterans continue to struggle with the disastrous Cerner medical records system at Spokane and Walla Walla VA hospitals. Farmers and ranchers face tariffs and rising fuel costs, while small businesses, workers, seniors, and young people are ignored by both parties.

My military career lasted 26 years, including deployments to Iraq and Afghanistan, where my son and I served at the same time. I planned to retire quietly in my small log cabin in Stevens County. I live simply, heat with a woodstove, garden and travel occasionally for my small business. Generally, I live on my military pension.

America is at a crossroads. We must defend the entire Bill of Rights, restore trust in government, uphold rule of law, strengthen military alliances, support free trade and a balanced budget and create opportunity for everyone — not just the wealthy and well-connected.

Eastern Washington needs a moderate leader who listens and represents us; the people. I am traveling across the district, listening to voters and learning your concerns firsthand. Supporting veterans, farmers, infrastructure, broadband expansion, free trade, and our allies abroad should not be partisan issues; they are fundamental American values
