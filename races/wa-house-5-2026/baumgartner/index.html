1 Voters’ Pamphlet Statement (General Election 2026)

Elected Experience

US Congressman (2025-); Treasurer, Spokane County (2019- 2025); Washington State Senator (2011-2018).

Other Professional Experience

Former Advisor, Hecla Mining. During the 2007 “surge”, I joined the State Department In Iraq. In 2009 I worked on a civilian counternarcotics team in Afghanistan, supporting farmers to grow wheat. (It’s where I met my wife, Eleanor.) We saw firsthand the bravery and sacrifices of our military. With WSU coach Mike Leach, I taught a class on football and counterinsurgency strategy.

Education

Gonzaga Prep and Pullman High; WSU – BA, Economics (1999); Harvard – MPA (2002).

Community Service

Former WSU Medical School Advisory Council. Active member St. Augustine’s Parish.

Statement

Today, Eastern Washington families are still being squeezed by high prices, federal overspending, and crime.

During my first term in Congress, I have kept my promise to help secure the border, reduce taxes, and cut waste and fraud. There is more work to do. 

I sponsored legislation to prohibit Members of Congress from trading individual stocks and profiting from special knowledge. I voted for permanent tax relief for working families and fought for lower taxes, smaller government, and more freedom. I helped enact laws to increase military salaries, and I will keep fighting for Fairchild Air Force Base and our veterans. I voted for the SAVE Act requiring people to show proof of citizenship when they register to vote, and supported a new $50 billion fund for rural healthcare. I helped pass the Farm Bill through the House for the first time in eight years, including my amendment focused on Eastern Washington water resources. I’ve worked to protect our Snake River dams. 

Public service means helping people. My office has answered over 180,000 emails and phone calls, helped hundreds of people cut through red tape, and returned $3.8 million to local families. I will always fight for our values while also working across the aisle when it helps Eastern Washington, treating people with respect and pushing to restore integrity and civility to Congress.

I’m proud to be supported by agriculture leaders like Mark Schoesler, Mary Dye and Alex McGregor, community leaders including Mike Padden and Phil Altmeyer, business leaders like Duane Vaagen and Barry Baker, sheriffs from Ferry, Stevens and Walla Walla Counties, and many more.

I love Eastern Washington: I grew up in Colton and on the Palouse, and my wife and I are raising our five children in Spokane. I would be honored to have your vote.

Finding 7.1: Town Hall Medicaid Assurance Preceded Vote for Bill Cutting Medicaid Funding HIGH

What happened
At a Spokane town hall in March 2025, Baumgartner told constituents there would be "no reduction in overall dollars to Medicaid." In May 2025 he voted for H.R. 1, the 2025 budget reconciliation law. Reporting based on Congressional Budget Office estimates described the law as reducing federal Medicaid spending by roughly $1 trillion over a decade through new work reporting requirements. The gap between the town hall assurance and the recorded vote is material in a district with heavy Apple Health enrollment.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
High
Defense
Baumgartner has framed the law as slowing the growth of Medicaid rather than cutting current dollars, and has described the changes as a first step toward broader entitlement reform. He maintains that overall program dollars are not reduced from current levels.
