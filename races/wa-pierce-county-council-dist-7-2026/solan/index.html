Finding 5.1: Reached the ballot only after a court struck down the county's registration rule MODERATE

What happened
Solan first registered to vote in Pierce County on March 26, 2026, and the Pierce County Elections Office ruled him ineligible on May 7, 2026 under County Charter Section 4.30, which requires councilmembers to be registered voters of their district for at least one year before filing. He had lived in the county since April 2022. Solan sued, and on May 18, 2026 Judge Shelly K. Moss of Pierce County Superior Court ordered him restored to the ballot, finding the one-year residency requirement already met the county's interest in candidate familiarity and that the added voter-registration requirement was not necessary. Voters weighing a candidate for local office may consider that he had been registered to vote in the district for only weeks when he filed and needed a court order to appear on the ballot.

Defense
Solan met the charter's residency requirement, having lived in the district since 2022, and won his case. His attorneys argued the voter-registration requirement added a qualification for office beyond what the Washington Constitution and Legislature allow, and the court agreed. Winning the case, rather than being disqualified, is the operative outcome.

Finding 5.2: As Seattle police union president, faced resignation calls over January 6 remarks MODERATE

What happened
Solan led the Seattle Police Officers Guild from 2020 until February 2026. In January 2021, after the U.S. Capitol attack, he drew calls to resign from eight of Seattle's nine City Council members over public remarks that appeared to connect Black Lives Matter protests to the events at the Capitol. He declined to resign and attributed the criticism to cancel culture. His tenure also included scrutiny in 2023 over released body-camera audio of a Seattle officer's remarks about the death of Jaahnavi Kandula; Solan said the audio was horrifying without context. A candidate running primarily on public safety brings a documented, contested record of union leadership that voters may weigh.

Defense
Solan was elected SPOG president by a wide margin and served two terms, and the guild's members repeatedly backed his approach. The Kandula audio involved another officer's words, not his own, and he publicly called that audio horrifying.
