Finding 5.1: Placed on the ballot by Superior Court order after the county elections office found him ineligible MODERATE

What happened
According to Gig Harbor Now, the Pierce County Elections Office notified Solan on May 7, 2026 that county records showed he first registered to vote on March 26, 2026, and that this made him ineligible under County Charter Section 4.30, which requires councilmembers to have been registered voters of their district for at least one year before filing. Solan petitioned Pierce County Superior Court for a writ of mandamus on May 8, 2026 (cause no. 26-2-08439-0, Michael Solan v. Linda Farmer). On May 18, 2026 Judge Shelly K. Moss entered an order granting Solan's emergency motion, and the court closed the case by order the same day. Gig Harbor Now reported that the order directed the Elections Office to place him on the ballot. The court docket shows no appeal through October 10, 2026.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
The court granted Solan's emergency motion and he was placed on the ballot. His attorneys argued that the voter-registration requirement adds a qualification for office beyond what the Washington Constitution and Legislature allow.

Finding 5.2: As Seattle police union president, faced resignation calls over January 6 remarks MODERATE

What happened
Solan led the Seattle Police Officers Guild from 2020 until February 2026. In January 2021, after the U.S. Capitol attack, he drew calls to resign from eight of Seattle's nine City Council members over public remarks that appeared to connect Black Lives Matter protests to the events at the Capitol. He declined to resign and attributed the criticism to cancel culture. His tenure also included scrutiny in 2023 over released body-camera audio of a Seattle officer's remarks about the death of Jaahnavi Kandula; Solan said the audio was horrifying without context.

Defense
Solan was elected SPOG president by a wide margin and served two terms, and the guild's members repeatedly backed his approach. He has said his January 2021 comments were mischaracterized and that he condemned the Capitol violence. The Kandula audio involved another officer's words, not his own, and he publicly called that audio horrifying.
