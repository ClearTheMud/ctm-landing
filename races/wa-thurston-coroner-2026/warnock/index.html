Finding 4.1: Public scrutiny of the coroner's office handling of the 2018 Yvonne McDonald death LOW

What happened
In August 2018, Yvonne McDonald, a 56-year-old Black woman, was found unconscious and partially clothed in a yard on Division Street Northwest in Olympia and died that evening at Providence St. Peter Hospital. Warnock's office conducted an autopsy, found no signs of strangulation, stab wounds or gunshot wounds, and sent toxicology and physical evidence to the Washington State Patrol. After public pressure, Warnock classified the death as suspicious, stating that the classification was a way to investigate the death rather than a determination that a crime had occurred. McDonald's family said they believed she was murdered, reported that she had bruising and internal injuries, and expressed frustration that the office took time to issue the suspicious ruling. The case drew sustained regional media coverage and a public campaign, Justice for Yvonne. The criminal investigation was led by the Olympia Police Department.

Defense
Warnock's office performed an autopsy, ordered state toxicology and crime-lab testing, and ruled the death suspicious, which is the classification that keeps a death investigation open. The criminal investigation was conducted by the Olympia Police Department, not the coroner. The family's concern centered on the pace and on transparency, not on a documented legal violation. No disciplinary action, official complaint finding, or court ruling against Warnock resulted. The events are from 2018.
