Statement

Thank you for the confidence you have placed in me as your Thurston County Coroner. It has been an honor to serve this community since 2007, and to work alongside dedicated professionals who respond with care, compassion, and professionalism during some of the most difficult moments families will ever face.

My work in this field began long before I was elected. I served as a funeral director and embalmer before joining the Coroner’s Office as Deputy Coroner in 2001. That experience shaped my commitment to treating every person with dignity, supporting families with respect, and ensuring death investigations are handled thoroughly and professionally.

Over the years, our office has grown to meet the needs of a changing county, including increased demands related to population growth, homelessness, overdoses, and complex death investigations. We have strengthened training, improved operations, invested in equipment, restored the Chief Deputy Coroner position for the first time since the 1990s, and, in 2025, proudly achieved state certification and national accreditation.

As Thurston County continues to grow and county offices face significant budget pressures, I remain committed to leading a compassionate, professional, and fiscally responsible Coroner’s Office. Thank you for allowing me to continue serving Thurston County.

Finding 5.1: Public scrutiny of the coroner's office handling of the 2018 Yvonne McDonald death LOW

What happened
In August 2018, Yvonne McDonald, a 56-year-old Black woman, was found unconscious and partially clothed in a yard on Division Street Northwest in Olympia and died that evening at Providence St. Peter Hospital. Warnock's office conducted an autopsy, found no signs of strangulation, stab wounds or gunshot wounds, and sent toxicology and physical evidence to the Washington State Patrol. After public pressure, Warnock classified the death as suspicious, stating that the classification was a way to investigate the death rather than a determination that a crime had occurred. McDonald's family said they believed she was murdered, reported that she had bruising and internal injuries, and expressed frustration that the office took time to issue the suspicious ruling. The case drew sustained regional media coverage and a public campaign, Justice for Yvonne. The criminal investigation was led by the Olympia Police Department.

Defense
Warnock's office performed an autopsy, ordered state toxicology and crime-lab testing, and ruled the death suspicious, which is the classification that keeps a death investigation open. The criminal investigation was conducted by the Olympia Police Department, not the coroner. The family's concern centered on the pace and on transparency, not on a documented legal violation. No disciplinary action, official complaint finding, or court ruling against Warnock resulted. The events are from 2018.
