Finding 5.1: 2011 Officer-Involved Fatal Shooting — Justified but Litigated MODERATE

What happened
In June 2011, while serving as a Lewis County Sheriff's deputy, McKnight fatally shot Steve Peterson, a 33-year-old Napavine man, during a burglary response. Peterson had been reported armed with a knife and had used it to stab a front door and pickup truck hood. When McKnight confronted Peterson, the man refused to remove his hands from his pockets and charged the deputy. McKnight fired four times. No weapon was found on Peterson's body. Lewis County Prosecutor Jonathan Meyer ruled the shooting justified. McKnight was cleared by internal review and returned to patrol. The Peterson family subsequently filed a federal lawsuit, which was dismissed. While legally resolved, this incident is part of the public record and may be raised by opponents or during forums.

Finding 5.3: Not Currently a Sheriff's Office Employee MODERATE

What happened
McKnight left the Lewis County Sheriff's Office after approximately six years and has been with the Chehalis Police Department for the remainder of his career. While his Chehalis PD experience as deputy chief brings management and budgetary credentials, opponents from within LCSO (Frase, Riordan) may argue he lacks current knowledge of the sheriff's office culture, staffing, and operational challenges. The transition from a city police department to a county sheriff's office — which includes corrections, civil process, court security, and patrol across 2,436 square miles — is a significant operational scope change.
