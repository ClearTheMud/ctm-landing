Finding 6.1: Fired by Centralia PD in 2026 after one of three policy allegations was sustained; arbitration pending HIGH

What happened
Centralia Police Department Notice of Disciplinary Decision I-26-01, dated April 29, 2026 and signed by Chief Andy Caldwell, terminated Murphy's employment after more than 30 years with the department. The matter began with an anonymous complaint to the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission (CJTC), received by the CJTC on January 31, 2026, alleging Murphy had made statements that the sitting Lewis County sheriff acquired and protected cocaine dealers. The people named in the complaint denied the claims to The Chronicle. The Notice resolved three policy allegations. Allegation 1 (unauthorized disclosure of protected information) and Allegation 2 (using his position to gain influence for non-department activity) were not sustained. For each, the Chief wrote that he was not convinced the allegation was unfounded but that the preponderance of the evidence did not support a sustained finding. Allegation 3 (giving false or misleading statements, or omitting material information, in an investigation) was sustained, based on Murphy's March 16 and March 23, 2026 interviews with an outside investigator. Murphy had been placed on paid administrative leave on March 24, 2026. The Chronicle reported on September 18, 2026 that his union grievance is scheduled for arbitration on November 19 and 20, 2026. The arbitration has not taken place and no decision has been issued.

Defense
Murphy denies providing false or misleading information. In his April 27, 2026 Loudermill statement, recorded in the Notice, he wrote: "At no time did I provide false or misleading information, nor did I omit material facts." He has attributed the differences between his interviews to the "competing obligations" of his roles as a Centralia sergeant and a DEA task force officer and group supervisor, and said the first interview "lacked clarity regarding terminology and scope." The Notice rejects these explanations. His union, Teamsters Local 252, has said the complaint is "substantially rooted in a contested election" and that the city "failed to provide credible evidence to establish just cause," and has asked that he be returned to duty.

Finding 6.2: Lewis County prosecutor issued a potential impeachment disclosure (Brady letter) HIGH

What happened
On May 13, 2026, Lewis County Prosecutor Jonathan Meyer issued a potential impeachment disclosure (PID), informally called a Brady letter, regarding Murphy, according to The Chronicle, which obtained and quoted the document. The Chronicle describes a PID as a notice used to disclose to a criminal defendant information about a government witness that may be exculpatory or impeaching. As quoted by The Chronicle, Meyer wrote that Murphy's explanation for the differences between his interviews fails, that even if Murphy had a duty not to disclose information "such a duty does not provide license to mislead," and that Murphy's actions were "material that must be disclosed." Meyer also wrote that the disclosure "should not be construed as any type of official determination by this office as to the relevance or admissibility of any of the information," and that any use of it at a hearing or trial may be the subject of pre-trial litigation. The Chronicle reported on September 18, 2026 that the letter does not prohibit Murphy from being elected sheriff. The PID document itself was not reviewed for this profile; its contents here are as quoted by The Chronicle.

Defense
Murphy has said the discrepancies came "from information that I have that pertain to federal investigations that are not JNET investigations," tied to a nondisclosure agreement he signed with the DEA, and that a later interview addressed the first interview's lack of clarity. He denies giving false or misleading information and is contesting his termination through arbitration.
