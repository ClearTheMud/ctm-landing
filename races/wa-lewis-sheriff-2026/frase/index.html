Finding 2.1: Insider Knowledge but Zero Public Campaign Presence HIGH

What happened
Frase is currently Special Services Chief at the Lewis County Sheriff's Office, giving him deep institutional knowledge and direct oversight of investigations, civil process, records, and evidence. However, as of May 2026, he has no detectable public campaign presence — no campaign website, no social media campaign page, no media interviews, and did not attend the April 2 Chronicle candidate forum. He was not mentioned in any Chronicle or Herald candidate coverage. This near-invisible campaign profile indicates he has not established visible campaign infrastructure.

Finding 2.2: 20+ Year LCSO Veteran — Deep Institutional Ties MODERATE

What happened
With over 20 years at the Lewis County Sheriff's Office, Frase has the longest tenure within LCSO of any current candidate. He rose from detective to Detective Sergeant to Special Services Chief, and participates in budget presentations to county commissioners. This deep institutional connection is both a strength (knows the office inside and out) and a vulnerability (could be seen as continuity with Sheriff Snaza's administration at a time when multiple candidates are campaigning on change).

Finding 2.3: Active LCSO Employee Running Against Colleagues LOW

What happened
Frase is an active-duty Special Services Chief running for sheriff while his colleague Danny Riordan (Sergeant) is also running from within LCSO. This creates internal organizational dynamics — a command-level officer and a sergeant from the same agency competing for the top position while the incumbent retires. Workplace dynamics and potential conflicts of interest during the campaign period are a concern.
