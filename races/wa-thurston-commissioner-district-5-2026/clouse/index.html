Finding 6.1: Censured by Fellow Commissioners Over Relationship with Executive Aide SEVERE

What happened
In December 2024, the Thurston County Board of Commissioners voted to censure Clouse after an investigation revealed she had a personal relationship with executive aide Allyn Hershey. The censure cited concerns about the power dynamic between an elected official and a subordinate staff member. Clouse publicly stated “I deeply regret my decision.” The Thurston County settlement related to the matter was approximately $300,000.
Source tier
T2 (multi-source: The Daily Chronicle, The Spokesman-Review)
Political impact
Severe — Most significant vulnerability in her re-election campaign. Demonstrates ethical lapse involving power dynamics with subordinate staff.
Defense
Clouse publicly acknowledged the relationship and expressed regret. She has continued to serve as commissioner and maintained her policy agenda. She won her initial race with nearly 60% of the vote, suggesting a strong base of support.
  • The Daily Chronicle: Commissioner censure investigation
  • The Spokesman-Review: Thurston County commissioner censured

Finding 6.2: $300,000 County Settlement Cost to Taxpayers SEVERE

What happened
The settlement related to the Hershey matter cost Thurston County taxpayers approximately $300,000. In the context of a county facing a $23.8 million structural budget deficit — the same deficit that forced the Clerk’s Office to close to the public — this settlement amount carries particular political weight.
Source tier
T2 (multi-source: The Daily Chronicle)
Political impact
Severe — Opponents will argue Clouse’s personal conduct directly cost the county resources it could not afford to lose during a budget crisis.
Defense
Settlements are common in employment disputes and the county’s legal counsel determined settlement was in the county’s best interest. The $300K represents a small fraction of the county’s overall budget.
  • The Daily Chronicle: Thurston County settlement details

Finding 6.3: Recall Petition Filed and Dismissed — Sustained Public Controversy HIGH

What happened
Following the censure, a recall petition was filed against Clouse. The petition was dismissed by Thurston County Superior Court, and the Washington Supreme Court subsequently rejected an attempt to unblock the recall. While Clouse was legally vindicated, the recall attempt indicates sustained organized opposition and keeps the ethics controversy in public discourse heading into 2026.
Source tier
T1 (court records) + T2 (The Daily Chronicle, The Spokesman-Review)
Political impact
High — Both the Superior Court and WA Supreme Court sided with Clouse, but the recall attempt itself demonstrates depth of community opposition.
Defense
Both courts sided with Clouse, indicating the recall petition did not meet the legal threshold. The dismissal reinforces that the censure was the appropriate remedy and no further action was warranted under law.
  • The Daily Chronicle: Recall petition dismissed
  • The Spokesman-Review: WA Supreme Court rejects recall attempt

Finding 6.4: Self-Described Democratic Socialist and Abolitionist — Polarizing Labels HIGH

What happened
Clouse has publicly identified as both a “democratic socialist” and an “abolitionist” (in the criminal justice context) through her Run For Something profile. While these positions align with her progressive base, they provide significant opposition research material. The abolitionist label may be difficult to defend in a county that includes rural and moderate suburban communities outside Olympia.
Source tier
T2 (Run For Something candidate directory)
Political impact
High — Provides clear attack surface in a general election context.
Defense
Clouse would argue these labels reflect principled commitment to equity and justice reform. She founded CARE to act on these values. Thurston County leans progressive (Biden won 59%) and she won her 2023 race with 60% despite these positions being publicly known.
