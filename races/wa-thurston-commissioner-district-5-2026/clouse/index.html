Statement

As a U.S. Army veteran, nonprofit leader, family business manager, and your County Commissioner, I believe government must be transparent, fiscally responsible, and accountable. When decisions affect your life, you deserve clear information and a seat at the table. That’s why I voted against a county budget process that bypassed community input while cutting staff and domestic violence navigation services.

In my first term, I have fought for results. As Chair of the Regional Housing Council, I oversaw approval of $25 million for affordable housing, including 186 new supportive units to prevent homelessness. I will continue prioritizing affordability at all income levels.

I have championed environmental investments that improve daily life for working families, including discounted heat pump upgrades and $7.6 million for the Deschutes Estuary restoration. From cleaner air to data-driven public safety, I pursue measurable, meaningful change.

Strong communities are built through responsible budgeting, economic opportunity, environmental stewardship, and public safety. That’s why I build coalitions across neighborhoods, industries, and backgrounds: because lasting solutions require everyone at the table. Together, we can continue building a Thurston County that is truly by and for the people. Endorsed by city and county officials, labor, and more. clouseforcommissioner.com

Finding 7.1: Censured by fellow commissioners over relationship with executive aide SEVERE

What happened
In December 2024, the Thurston County Board of Commissioners censured Clouse after a workplace investigation into her personal relationship with executive aide Allyn Hershey. The board and the investigation cited the power dynamic between an elected official and a subordinate she had hired. The county agreed to pay the former aide a $300,000 settlement. Clouse stated publicly, "I deeply regret my decision."
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Severe
Defense
Clouse acknowledged the relationship publicly and expressed regret. She continued to serve as commissioner and won her initial 2023 race with nearly 60% of the vote. Supporters may argue the censure was the appropriate accountability mechanism.

Finding 7.2: Recall petition over the aide matter filed, then dismissed by the courts HIGH

What happened
Arthur West, an Olympia open-government advocate, filed a petition on December 12, 2024 to recall Clouse over the Hershey matter. The charges alleged she hired someone with whom she had a personal relationship, continued the relationship afterward, accepted money and items for personal use, and violated county ethics policy. Thurston County Superior Court dismissed the petition, and in February 2026 the Washington Supreme Court affirmed the dismissal, holding that none of the charges were both factually and legally sufficient to support a recall.
Source tier
T1
Political impact
High
Defense
Both the Superior Court and the state Supreme Court found the charges did not meet the legal standard for a recall, so no recall proceeded. Clouse can point to the rulings as a judicial resolution in her favor.
