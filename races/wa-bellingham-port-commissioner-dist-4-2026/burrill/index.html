Finding 5.1: Seafood logistics and trucking businesses overlap with the port operations he would oversee MODERATE

What happened
Burrill is a shareholder in a seafood company and owner of an interstate trucking company, and his stated top priority is expanding the Port of Bellingham's cargo capacity, including lengthening the Bellingham International Airport runway to move Alaska seafood by air. A Port of Bellingham commissioner sets policy and budgets for the airport, marine terminals, and waterfront, the same facilities that serve the seafood shipping and freight sectors his businesses operate in. He currently chairs the port's Marine Advisory Committee. Voters and the port would need his business interests disclosed and, where required, recusals on matters that affect them.

Political impact

Defense
Burrill presents his 30 years in commercial fishing and seafood logistics as direct qualification for the seat, arguing his industry experience helps him protect the working waterfront and family-wage maritime jobs. Washington law provides recusal and disclosure procedures for elected officials with financial interests, and advocating for a sector one knows well is common and lawful for port commissioners.
