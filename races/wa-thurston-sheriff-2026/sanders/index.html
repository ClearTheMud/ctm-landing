Finding 5.1: Christopher Burbank Hiring — Acquitted Officer in Manny Ellis Death SEVERE

What happened
In April 2024, Sanders hired Christopher Burbank, a former Tacoma police officer acquitted of manslaughter/murder charges in the 2020 in-custody death of Manuel Ellis, a Black man who died during restraint while pleading for breath. Significant community backlash followed. Burbank resigned two days later citing death threats to his family. Manny Ellis’s sister, who had moved to Thurston County for safety, was blindsided by the hire. Sanders held a public forum and ultimately admitted he ‘failed to consider the greater community impact.’
Source tier
T2 (multi-source: KOMO, CBS News, The Center Square)
Political impact
Severe — Demonstrates a pattern of judgment lapses on sensitive community issues. The public apology helps but the damage to trust with communities of color persists.
Defense
Sanders publicly apologized, admitted he was wrong, and pledged to rethink hiring decisions. Burbank was legally acquitted, and Sanders argued the hire was based on qualifications. He has since emphasized community-centered hiring standards.
  • KOMO News: Thurston County Sheriff hiring controversy
  • CBS News: Former Tacoma officer acquitted in Ellis death resigns new deputy job
  • The Center Square: Thurston County sheriff hires officer from Manny Ellis case

Finding 5.2: Pursuit-Related Deaths and Injuries — Five Incidents in 2024 HIGH

What happened
During the period Sanders’ pro-pursuit policy was in effect, at least five pursuit-related incidents occurred in 2024 resulting in deaths or serious injuries. On September 7, one person died and two were injured. On September 20, a motorcyclist died following a pursuit. On September 24, three people were injured. Nicole Romanoff was sentenced to 20 years for second-degree murder in connection with a September 2024 pursuit/crash. Formal complaints were filed against the department’s pursuit tactics. Sanders dismissed them as ‘meritless.’
Source tier
T2 (multi-source: The Chronicle, FOX 13, KOMO)
Political impact
High — Pattern of incidents under an aggressive pursuit policy that Sanders has doubled down on publicly.
Defense
Sanders argues pursuits are necessary for public safety, notes deputies voluntarily terminate more than half of pursuits initiated due to safety concerns, and invested in grapple nets and GPS trackers as alternatives. The fatalities involve suspect-caused crashes, not deputy actions directly.
  • The Chronicle: Sanders responds to string of injuries, fatalities following pursuits
  • FOX 13: Thurston WA sheriff says police pursuit complaints ‘meritless’
  • KOMO: Woman sentenced for killing bystander in 2024 police chase

Finding 5.3: Captain’s Association Accuses Sanders of Nepotism and Retaliation SEVERE

What happened
The Thurston County Sheriff’s Office Captain’s Association (TCSOCA) — his own command staff — endorsed his opponent for the first time in the association’s history, accusing Sanders of nepotism in promotions, favoritism over merit, retaliatory management, encouraging ‘bullying and mob rule,’ maintaining ‘sole control of the agency’s transparency and supervisory decisions,’ and being ‘a social media influencer, not a leader.’
Source tier
T2 (The Chronicle, with direct TCSOCA statement)
Political impact
Severe — First time TCSOCA has ever opposed a sitting incumbent. Command staff rebellion is an extraordinary indicator of internal dysfunction.
Defense
Sanders attributed the criticism to ‘three employees’ within the association and noted he secured them 15-22% pay increases and industry-leading retirement benefits. He claims the same group endorsed his opponent in 2022 as well.
  • The Chronicle: Burton-Crow earns Captain’s Association endorsement

Finding 5.4: Deputy-Involved Fatal Shooting During Campaign (April 2026) MODERATE

What happened
On April 28, 2026, TCSO deputies shot and killed Todd Bass, 44, of Lacey after a pursuit that began when a vehicle was clocked at approximately 90 mph in a 35 zone on Yelm Highway. The suspect fled on foot after crashing. Three deputies were involved. The WA Office of Independent Investigations (OII) is leading the case.
Source tier
T1 (OII investigation record) + T2 (KOMO, The Chronicle)
Political impact
Moderate — Timing during campaign period draws scrutiny. OII investigation ongoing; no findings released.
Defense
The OII investigation is ongoing and no findings have been released. Deputies reported hearing shots fired before engaging. The suspect was fleeing a high-speed pursuit posing danger to the public.
  • KOMO: Suspect killed after chase, deputy-involved shooting in Thurston County
  • The Chronicle: TCSO deputy fatally shoots suspect following pursuit

Finding 5.5: Jail Conditions — Fentanyl Deaths, Overcrowding, Booking Restrictions MODERATE

What happened
Under Sanders’ tenure, the jail has experienced fentanyl smuggling leading to at least one inmate death, chronic overcrowding (average daily population ~425), booking restrictions for female inmates due to space constraints, and an inmate assault on a deputy. Sanders has publicly warned the county is ‘one step away from being sued’ over conditions.
Source tier
T2 (multi-source: The Chronicle, Seattle Red)
Political impact
Moderate — Overcrowding predates Sanders but persists under his watch. Fentanyl deaths and booking restrictions are concrete outcomes.
Defense
Sanders blames SB 5695 (banning routine strip searches and limiting body scanners) for inability to detect smuggled drugs. He has formed a work group with county commissioners and advocates for facility expansion. The overcrowding predates his tenure and reflects systemic county underfunding.
  • The Chronicle: Thurston County forms work group to address jail space
  • The Chronicle: Thurston County jail enacts booking restrictions for women
  • Seattle Red: Thurston County drug inmate overdose

Finding 5.6: Relative Inexperience — Youngest Sheriff with Limited LE Background MODERATE

What happened
Sanders was elected at age 28 with only 6 years of law enforcement experience as a deputy. He had no supervisory or management experience before becoming the top law enforcement officer for 150,000 residents.
Source tier
T1 (election records) + T2 (KING 5, Ballotpedia)
Political impact
Moderate — Mitigated by nearly 4 years in office and demonstrable accomplishments (body cameras, HQ, voter-approved funding).
Defense
Sanders successfully defeated a 3-term incumbent by 10 points, has hired 142 applicants while maintaining high standards, secured body cameras, a new headquarters, and voter-approved funding. His youth was an asset in the 2022 election and voters chose generational change.
