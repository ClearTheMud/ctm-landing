Finding 5.1: Limited Law Enforcement Experience — 5 Years Full-Time HIGH

What happened
Burton-Crow has only 5 years of full-time sworn law enforcement experience (since March 16, 2021). He has never held a supervisory or management rank in law enforcement — never a sergeant, lieutenant, or captain. He is running for the top position (County Sheriff overseeing 150,000 residents) without any formal command experience in policing. His 6 years as a volunteer reserve deputy (2014-2020) were part-time and unpaid.
Source tier
T1 (WA SOS filing records) + T2 (The Chronicle)
Political impact
High — Core vulnerability. However, the incumbent Sanders also had only 6 years of experience when elected.
Defense
Burton-Crow counters with 20 years of military leadership as a Sergeant First Class (E-7), including combat deployments to Afghanistan, Iraq, and UAE. He holds two master’s degrees (MPA and MS Criminal Justice). The TCSOCA endorsement demonstrates command staff confidence in his leadership ability. The incumbent, Sanders, also had only 6 years of experience when elected.

Finding 5.2: SB 5974 Eligibility Challenge — Near-Disqualification MODERATE

What happened
Incumbent Sanders publicly attempted to disqualify Burton-Crow under SB 5974, which requires 5 years of continuous full-time law enforcement experience. Sanders claimed Burton-Crow would be ‘approximately three months short’ of the requirement. Though Burton-Crow refuted this (his sworn-in date of March 16, 2021 gives him 5 years before the filing deadline), and the law was subsequently blocked by a Thurston County Superior Court injunction, the episode highlighted the thinness of his experience margin.
Source tier
T1 (WA SOS, court records) + T2 (The Chronicle, WA State Standard)
Political impact
Moderate — Resolved in Burton-Crow’s favor. Law blocked as likely unconstitutional. Sanders’ challenge was seen by some as politically motivated.
Defense
Burton-Crow provided documentation of his March 16, 2021 sworn-in date, establishing 5 full years before the 2026 filing deadline. The law itself was blocked as likely unconstitutional. Sanders’ challenge was seen by some as politically motivated rather than substantive.

Finding 5.3: Running Against His Own Boss — Workplace Dynamic Risks MODERATE

What happened
Burton-Crow is a current TCSO employee (Special Victims Detective) running against the elected sheriff who is his boss. This creates potential for workplace retaliation, claims of insubordination, or perception of disloyalty within the department. The TCSOCA allegations of ‘retaliatory management’ by Sanders amplify this concern. Burton-Crow must work under Sanders’ command through the election.
Source tier
T2 (The Chronicle)
Political impact
Moderate — Mitigated by the fact that Sanders himself ran the exact same campaign (deputy challenging his own sheriff) in 2022.
Defense
Burton-Crow’s union presidency (TCDSA) provides some labor-law protections against retaliation. Multiple command staff (TCSOCA) publicly support him, indicating the internal opposition is broader than one individual. The incumbent, Sanders, ran the exact same campaign — a deputy challenging his own sheriff (Snaza) — and won.
