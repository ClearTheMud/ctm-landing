Both candidates advance to the November general election

Sheriff is a partisan office, and Washington uses a top-two primary. Only two candidates filed for this race, so both Derek Sanders and Kevin Burton-Crow move on to the November 3, 2026 general election no matter how the August 4 primary turns out. The primary result is a measure of standing, not an elimination.

How to read these counts

Finding counts are not comparable between candidates. How much of a person’s conduct becomes public record depends heavily on the job they hold. A sitting sheriff generates a continuous public record through press coverage, incident reports, union grievances and records requests. A detective generates very little by default. A higher count can reflect greater public exposure rather than greater concern, and a lower count is not a clearance.

We publish a finding only when it is materially relevant to a voter and sourced at T1 (Official Record) or T2 (Multi-Source Media). Concerns that reduce to a fundraising disadvantage, a gap in experience, a routine dynamic of the race itself, or a dispute that was resolved in the candidate’s favor are reviewed and then set aside. Both candidates in this race are held to that same standard. See our methodology for the full criteria.

2 Race Dynamics

Detective running against his own boss, the same pattern Sanders used to defeat incumbent Snaza in 2022. The Captain’s Association endorsed the challenger for the first time in history, citing nepotism, favoritism, and retaliatory management. Key issues: Christopher Burbank hiring controversy, pursuit-related fatalities, jail overcrowding, and SB 5974 eligibility law.

Endorsements. Sanders has 12 recorded endorsements, most of them from elected officials: Lt. Gov. Denny Heck, U.S. Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, state Sen. Jessica Bateman, state Rep. Lauren Davis, and all three Thurston County commissioners, along with the Tumwater Police Guild, Tumwater Firefighters IAFF Local 2409, the Thurston County Democratic Party, former Thurston County sheriff and commissioner Gary Edwards, and Clark County Sheriff Mike Morrison. Burton-Crow has three: the Thurston County Sheriff’s Office Captain’s Association, state Sen. John Lovick (a former state trooper and former Snohomish County sheriff), and the Thurston-Lewis-Mason Central Labor Council.

Endorsement counts are not a measure of support among voters. An incumbent accumulates institutional endorsements that a challenger rarely has access to, so the totals reflect incumbency as much as anything else. Note also that the Thurston County Democratic Party endorsed Sanders, who prefers no party, over Burton-Crow, who prefers the Democratic Party.

SB 5974 and ballot eligibility. SB 5974 (Chapter 258, Laws of 2026) set new qualifications for county sheriff candidates, including five years of law enforcement experience, a minimum age of 25, and peace officer certification. Four eastern Washington sheriffs sued the state over the law. That case was not about this race. On April 29, 2026, one day before the law was to take effect and one day before candidate filing week, Thurston County Superior Court Judge Christine Schaller granted a preliminary injunction blocking parts of it as they apply to sheriffs. On May 5, 2026, a Washington Supreme Court deputy commissioner denied the state’s emergency request to lift that injunction, leaving it in place while the litigation continues. The case has not been finally decided.

Separately, during the campaign Sheriff Sanders said publicly that the five-year requirement would disqualify Burton-Crow. Burton-Crow responded that he was sworn in at the Thurston County Sheriff’s Office on March 16, 2021. No court or election official ruled on his eligibility. He filed and was certified to the August 4, 2026 primary ballot. This is recorded as race background. Neither the lawsuit nor the campaign exchange is published as a finding against either candidate.

July 11 debate. The two candidates met in a two-hour virtual debate on July 11, 2026, hosted by Tony Taylor of the “Leaders Lead” podcast. Both named the county jail as their top priority for the next term. Sanders proposed adding a mental health wing to move mentally ill offenders out of the general population rather than building more jail space. Burton-Crow did not offer a specific jail plan and questioned why the issue was not addressed in Sanders’ first term, saying spending on capital improvements “outweighed some of the needs” and calling for more spending on new-hire training, which currently runs nine weeks at entry level. They also differed on whether the sheriff’s office should seek professional accreditation and on whether the sheriff belongs in the field or behind a desk. Reported by The Chronicle, July 13, 2026.

3 Corrections

2026-07-26, corrected the endorsement summary and updated campaign finance

This page previously said that Burton-Crow held “the internal credibility endorsements (Captain’s Association, rank-and-file union, Sen. Lovick).” That summary was wrong in two ways. It left out the Thurston-Lewis-Mason Central Labor Council, a tri-county labor body that is not internal to the sheriff’s office. And it listed a rank-and-file union endorsement that does not appear in our records at all; Burton-Crow is president of the deputy sheriff’s association, which is a role he holds rather than an endorsement he received. Both candidates’ endorsements are now listed in full.

Campaign finance figures on this page were also stale. They previously showed $54,723 for Sanders and $36,317 for Burton-Crow, which came from an older collection and carried no date. Current Public Disclosure Commission records show $81,383 for Sanders through July 20 and $45,253 for Burton-Crow through July 21. All figures on this page now carry the date they are current as of.

2026-07-26, corrected our description of SB 5974 and ballot eligibility

Earlier the same day, this page said that SB 5974’s “application to this race was challenged,” that “the law was blocked in court,” and that “Kevin Burton-Crow was confirmed eligible for the ballot.” All three were wrong or imprecise, and we have replaced that passage.

The lawsuit was brought by four eastern Washington sheriffs as a challenge to the statute itself. It was not about this race and not about Burton-Crow. The court did not strike the law down; it granted a preliminary injunction against parts of it, which a Washington Supreme Court deputy commissioner then declined to lift. The litigation is still open. And no court or election official ever ruled on Burton-Crow’s eligibility, because no formal challenge to it was ever filed. He filed and was certified like any other candidate.

We introduced this error ourselves while rewriting the section, by sharpening older internal notes into published prose without checking them against the court record. It was live for part of one day. We have since verified each element against the bill record and contemporaneous reporting.
