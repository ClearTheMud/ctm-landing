Statement

I am a sixth generation Harborite, and I am running for County Auditor to bring accountability, accuracy, and a renewed commitment to our community. Grays Harbor deserves timely and precise audits, honest leadership, and an Auditor who is dependable, professional, and dedicated to serving the public.

The Auditor’s Office requires more than technical skills. It requires integrity and a commitment to responsible oversight. My career in financial services has been built on precision, regulatory compliance, and consistent follow-through. I understand how delayed audits, unclear records, or inconsistent communication can erode trust. Our community deserves better.

My focus is straightforward. Clear information, dependable service, and transparent processes that residents can rely on. I am committed to ensuring every detail, from financial oversight to elections and public records, is handled with accuracy, care, and respect for the people I serve. Grays Harbor needs an Auditor who is prepared, responsive, and steady in their work. For me, responsibility to this community is not a campaign message. It is a way of life.

I’m asking for your vote, so I can bring Community First, Integrity Always leadership to the Grays Harbor County Auditor’s Office.

Full list of endorsements is available at www.charleeforgraysharbor.com

Finding 6.1: No government or elections-administration experience MODERATE

What happened
Paull's entire 19-year career has been in private-sector banking and mortgage lending. She has no experience in government administration, elections management, property tax collection, vehicle or vessel licensing, or any of the county auditor's core functions. The county auditor role covers elections, financial accounting, recording, and licensing, none of which directly correspond to mortgage origination.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Paull says banking requires precision, integrity, and regulatory compliance, which she argues are transferable to auditor functions. Her mortgage lending work involves property records, title searches, and financial documentation that touch the auditor's domain. The departing auditor (MacLean) had government experience and still lost the DOL licensing contract.
