Finding 5.1: Named in litigation over Tacoma minimum-wage initiative timeline; court ruled her office followed the law MODERATE

What happened
Farmer's office was named in a 2025 lawsuit (Cause No. 25-2-10781-2) brought by UFCW Local 367, Tacoma for All, and the Tacoma Democratic Socialists of America. The plaintiffs alleged Pierce County delayed verification of signatures for a City of Tacoma initiative to raise the minimum wage to $20 per hour, and that the delay pushed the measure past the deadline for the November ballot. The complaint stated the county did not begin validating signatures until 13 days after they were submitted. The Pierce County Superior Court found the Auditor's Office acted with reasonable promptness in validating over 2,000 pages of signatures and properly followed state law, and ordered the measure onto the February 2026 ballot. The Court of Appeals, Division Two, later stayed that placement pending review.

Defense
The Superior Court ruled in the Auditor's favor, finding the office acted with reasonable promptness and followed state law. The dispute centered as much on the City of Tacoma's timing as on the county, and the primary legal question was one of statutory deadlines rather than misconduct by the auditor.
