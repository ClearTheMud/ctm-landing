Statement

For the past 30 years, I have proudly called Clallam County home and dedicated 36 years to serving in the Clallam and Jefferson County Auditor’s Offices. Since being elected County Auditor in 2015, I have focused on professionalism, accountability, transparency, and responsible stewardship of public resources across all divisions of the office while providing responsive and respectful public service.

The Auditor’s Office serves nearly every resident through elections, licensing, recording services, payroll and financial oversight. I believe these essential services should be accurate, accessible, efficient, and delivered with integrity, fairness, compassion, and respect for every resident.

Public trust matters. My priority has always been protecting voter confidence and ensuring every eligible citizen has the opportunity to register and vote. Throughout my 25 years in elections administration, I have been committed to transparent, secure, and accurate elections conducted fairly, impartially, and in full compliance with the United States Constitution, federal law, and the Constitution and laws of the State of Washington.

I am honored to be endorsed by Secretary of State Steve Hobbs and county auditors across Washington, and to have the support of former Secretary of State Kim Wyman.

I respectfully ask for your vote.

Finding 5.1: 125 ballots from the 2016 general election went uncounted MODERATE

What happened
On January 25, 2017, the Clallam County elections office found 125 unopened ballots from the November 8, 2016 general election in a courthouse parking lot drop box while preparing for a February special election. The ballots could not be counted because the election had already been certified. The county elections supervisor resigned the next day, and Riggs said she would hire a replacement. Riggs also said her office had not been following state law, in that election and in past elections, by not recording the time ballots were removed from drop boxes. She asked the Washington Secretary of State's Office to evaluate the elections office's procedures and practices. Election administration is the core duty of the auditor's office.
Source tier
T2
Political impact
Moderate
Defense
Riggs said she bore ultimate responsibility as auditor and released a public report on the incident ("Final Report Regarding 125 Uncounted Ballots"). She said she did not ask for the supervisor's resignation. She said her office would log the time ballots are removed from drop boxes and would perform a final check of ballot boxes after each election, a step suggested by the Secretary of State's Office. The 125 ballots would not have changed the result of any race on the November 2016 ballot.
