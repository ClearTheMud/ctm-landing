1 Background

Daniel Kleban, 48, of Cumberland, is a co-founder and co-owner of Maine Beer Company. He earned an undergraduate degree from the University of Southern Maine (2004) and a law degree from Boston College Law School (2007), then worked at a Portland law firm. After being laid off during the 2009 recession, he and his wife Beth cashed out their retirement savings to start Maine Beer Company with his brother David in 2009. The company began as a one-barrel operation in Portland, later relocated to Freeport, and grew to more than 9,000 barrels of annual production (reported 2015). Its motto, "Do What's Right," has carried into Kleban's campaign branding. No labor, environmental-enforcement, or business-litigation controversy involving the company was identified in the public record.

Kleban has never held elected office. His only prior candidacy was the 2025 Senate run he suspended after 41 days. His experience level is a matter of public record and is noted here as context, not as an adverse finding. 2 Commitment Record

Finding 2.1: Suspended His Own Campaign After 41 Days, Then Re-entered the Same Race MODERATE

What happened
Kleban launched his Democratic US Senate campaign on September 3, 2025, with an affordability message ("the cost of living is too damn high"). He suspended it on October 14, 2025, after 41 days, and endorsed Gov. Janet Mills, who entered the primary the same day, saying "While our message was right, the timing isn't." After Graham Platner exited the race on July 8, 2026, Kleban re-entered the same day via a Substack post, saying he is "in to replace" Platner and calling for an open, transparent process to select a nominee over the following two weeks.
Kleban's response
He framed both the exit and the return under the Maine Beer Company motto "Do What's Right." His re-entry follows the field being reopened by Platner's departure rather than a broken pledge to stay out of the race.

3 Positioning: From Endorsing Mills to New-Generation Alternative

Finding 3.1: Endorsed the Establishment Candidate, Now Runs as the Fresh Alternative MODERATE

What happened
In October 2025 Kleban endorsed sitting Gov. Janet Mills as "the right leader for this moment." His July 2026 re-entry pitch emphasizes a new generation of leadership and an open nominating process. Both statements are on the public record. Mills later suspended her own campaign, and Platner became the presumptive nominee before exiting in July 2026.
Kleban's response
His endorsement and later re-entry each occurred as the field changed around him, with Mills entering and then suspending and Platner's exit reopening the race.

Finding 4.2: Business-Owner Interest in the Regulated Alcohol Industry MODERATE

What happened
Kleban co-owns Maine Beer Company, a federally and state-regulated alcohol producer. His prior campaign drew contributions from fellow brewery owners, including Sam Calagione of Dogfish Head, Gary Fish of Deschutes, and Charlie Papazian. A US Senator votes on alcohol excise taxes, Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB) regulation, and interstate shipping rules that directly affect the candidate's business and donor base.
