1 Character / Past Conduct

Finding 1.1: Nazi-Resembling Tattoo (Totenkopf) SEVERE

What happened
In October 2025, a video of Platner dancing shirtless at his brother's wedding surfaced, revealing a chest tattoo resembling the Totenkopf (death's head) symbol used by the Nazi SS. Platner said he got it in Croatia in 2007 while drunk with his Marine unit, picked from options on a wall. He says he was unaware of the Nazi connection and immediately had it covered up when it was pointed out.
Source tier

Defense

Finding 1.7: Named Woman Alleges 2021 Sexual Assault, Platner Categorically Denies SEVERE DENIED

The two had an on-and-off relationship beginning in 2019. Racicot also gave interviews to the Washington Post and CNN. 6 June 9 Primary Result & Post-Primary Landscape

Primary Result (UNOFFICIAL, Pending Maine SOS Certification)

Platner won the June 9, 2026 Democratic primary decisively. The race was called by Decision Desk HQ/Bangor Daily News at 9:07 p.m. and by the AP (carried by Maine Public) at approximately 9:25 p.m. On the most complete unofficial tabulation, Platner took 72.0%, with Janet Mills (suspended April 30 but still on the ballot) at 19.5% and David Costello at 8.0%. All percentages are unofficial pending Maine SOS certification. Victory statement: “We will take back this Senate seat and when we do we will take back our power.” (T2, multi-source media tabulation: Bangor Daily News, Maine Public, Al Jazeera.)

Party Establishment Calls on Platner to Withdraw (July 2026)

The post-primary picture reversed after July 6, 2026. Following the allegation in Finding 1.7, Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer and DSCC Chair Kirsten Gillibrand called on Platner to withdraw, and the DSCC said it “will not invest in the Maine Senate race if Platner remains on the ballot.” Others who called for him to step aside or withdrew endorsements include Sen. Elizabeth Warren, Sen. Ruben Gallego, Rep. Ro Khanna, Sen. Martin Heinrich, Rep. Rashida Tlaib, the Maine Democratic Party, and End Citizens United. Sen. Bernie Sanders, a longtime ally, privately urged him to step aside. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez did not join the calls, framing the race as a choice for Maine voters. An earlier post-primary statement in which Schumer and Gillibrand backed Platner (“In November, Maine voters will elect Graham Platner”) is superseded by these July 6 calls for his withdrawal. (T2, The Hill, Axios, Boston Globe, Al Jazeera.)

Ballot-Replacement Deadlines

Under Maine law (Title 21-A, section 374-A), Platner can be replaced on the general-election ballot only if he withdraws by 5:00 p.m. on Monday, July 13, 2026, in which case the Maine Democratic Party must name a replacement nominee by 5:00 p.m. on Monday, July 27, 2026. The party, not the state, selects any replacement, and state law does not set the method. As of July 7, 2026 Platner had not said whether he would withdraw. Names floated by reporters include former state Senate President Troy Jackson, who filed FEC exploratory paperwork, former Maine CDC director Nirav Shah, and Secretary of State Shenna Bellows. Rep. Jared Golden declined. (T1 statute; T2 reporting: CBS News, Newsweek, The Hill.)

Gov. Mills Declines to Endorse

On June 1, amid the explicit-messages story, Mills publicly reminded voters “I am still on the ballot” but did not resume campaigning. Post-primary, she declined to endorse Platner: her statement thanked supporters without mentioning him, and spokesperson Tommy Garcia said she has never voted for Collins and won’t, and “will continue to hear and watch how Graham Platner works to earn the support of Maine voters.” (T2, Portland Press Herald, NBC News, CNN.)
