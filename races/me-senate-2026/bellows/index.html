Shenna Bellows is Maine's 50th Secretary of State and the first woman to hold the office. She led the ACLU of Maine for eight years and served two terms in the Maine State Senate. She has never won a statewide election: she lost the 2014 US Senate race to Susan Collins and was eliminated in the 2026 Democratic gubernatorial primary. She entered the US Senate race on July 9, 2026, the day after Graham Platner suspended his campaign, and now seeks the replacement nomination at the July 25, 2026 party convention. Her defining public act is the 2023 decision, later overturned unanimously by the US Supreme Court, that removed Donald Trump from Maine's 2024 primary ballot. If nominated she would face the same incumbent who defeated her by 36 points in 2014.

1 Background and Record

Finding 1.1: Career built in advocacy and appointed office, not statewide election MODERATE

What happened
Bellows was Executive Director of the ACLU of Maine from 2005 to 2013 and later led the Holocaust and Human Rights Center of Maine. She served two terms in the Maine State Senate (2016 to 2020), then was elected Maine Secretary of State by the Legislature and sworn in January 4, 2021, becoming the 50th holder of the office and the first woman to hold it. Under Maine's constitution the Secretary of State is chosen by joint ballot of the Legislature, not by statewide vote, so she has held the office since 2021 by vote of the Democratic-controlled Legislature rather than by the electorate.

2 The 2023 Trump Ballot Decision

This is the central item in Bellows' public record. It is presented here as a documented official act and its reversal, not as wrongdoing.

Finding 2.1: The 14th Amendment ballot removal, unanimously overturned by the US Supreme Court CRITICAL

What happened
On December 28, 2023, acting as Secretary of State, Bellows issued a 34-page decision ruling Donald Trump ineligible for Maine's 2024 Republican primary ballot under Section 3 of the 14th Amendment (the insurrection clause), based on his conduct around January 6, 2021. On March 4, 2024, the US Supreme Court ruled unanimously in Trump v. Anderson that states cannot enforce Section 3 against federal candidates, and Bellows rescinded her ruling the same day.
Reaction
Criticism came from both parties. Sen. Susan Collins and Rep. Jared Golden both said Trump should remain on the ballot absent a criminal conviction, and Maine Republican legislators filed impeachment demands. Rep. Chellie Pingree (D) and Rep. Bill Pascrell (D-NJ) supported the reasoning.
Bellows' response
She has defended the decision as required by her oath of office and by the evidentiary record before her, and denied acting for partisan reasons. She rescinded the ruling the same day the Supreme Court decided the constitutional question.

Political impact
National Republican and independent voters can frame it as overreach by an official the electorate never chose, and it is the most likely line of attack from Collins in a rematch. Supporters frame it as principled enforcement of the Constitution.

3 Statewide Electoral Record

Finding 3.1: Two statewide losses, including a 36-point 2014 defeat to Collins HIGH

What happened
In 2014 Bellows was the Democratic nominee against incumbent Susan Collins and lost 67 percent to 31 percent (FEC candidate S4ME00071). In the June 2026 Democratic gubernatorial primary she placed fourth of five candidates with roughly 20.6 percent of first-choice votes and was eliminated in the ranked-choice rounds; Hannah Pingree won the primary.
