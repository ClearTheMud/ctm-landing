Bottom Line Up Front

David Costello is a convention contender to replace Graham Platner as the Democratic nominee against Susan Collins. He is a long-shot. His last two statewide runs both ended in low finishes: about 11 percent against Angus King in 2024 and a third-place 5.6 percent in the June 2026 primary. His case to convention delegates rests on roughly three decades of senior government management experience rather than electoral momentum. 1 Background and Career

Costello is a Bangor-born, Old Town-raised Democrat from a working-class Maine family. His father was an Army veteran and labor organizer, and his grandparents worked in shoe, textile, and railroad trades. He was educated at Old Town High School, George Washington University, and the London School of Economics.

He has roughly thirty years in senior government roles. Early on he worked as an aide in Maine, including for the Maine Secretary of State on elections and campaign-finance reform, and for the Maine State Senate. He then served as a USAID Country Program and Regional Team Leader, managing foreign-aid programs in conflict zones including Cambodia, Haiti, Bosnia, Croatia, Serbia, and Kosovo. In the 2000s he was a senior aide to the Mayor of Baltimore on schools and youth programs. From roughly 2007 to 2011 he was a senior aide to the Governor of Maryland, and from 2011 to 2015 he served as Deputy and Acting Secretary of the Maryland Department of the Environment, a role covering more than 1,000 employees and a $380 million annual budget. He later coordinated Maryland climate and clean-energy work. He centers this management record as his differentiator. (T2 media on the Maryland environment post; T3 campaign biography for the balance.)

On the residency question: Costello is Maine-born and Maine-raised, so there is no carpetbagger issue on birthplace. The only neutral context is career geography. His most senior executive service, roughly fifteen years, was out of state in Baltimore city and Maryland state government before he returned to Maine politics.

2 Electoral Record

Finding 2.1: Two consecutive weak statewide showings VIABILITY

What happened
Costello ran for US Senate in Maine in 2024 as a Democrat against independent incumbent Angus King and took roughly 11 percent to King's 52 percent. In the June 9, 2026 Democratic primary for Susan Collins's seat he finished third with about 5.6 percent, behind Graham Platner (77.7 percent) and Governor Janet Mills (16.7 percent). His 2024 and 2026 runs share a single FEC candidate record.
