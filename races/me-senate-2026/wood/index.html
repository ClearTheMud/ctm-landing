Candidate Dossier: Jordan Wood (D-ME)

2026 Maine US Senate, Democratic Nominating Convention

Jordan Wood is a campaign-finance-reform advocate and former Vice President of End Citizens United who served as chief of staff to Rep. Katie Porter (D-CA). He entered the 2026 Maine US Senate race, switched to the 2nd Congressional District US House primary, lost that primary in June 2026, and then returned to the Senate race. He is now one of the candidates seeking the Democratic nomination at the July 25, 2026 convention to replace Graham Platner. The nomination remains undecided.

1 Background & Record

Wood was born and raised in Maine, growing up in Lewiston and Gardiner in a working-class household, the son of a teacher and a pastor. He attended public schools and funded college with need-based loans, then moved to Washington, D.C. He served as Vice President of End Citizens United, a campaign-finance-reform group, where he built the "No Corporate PAC" pledge. In 2020 he became chief of staff to US Rep. Katie Porter (D-CA), and was in Porter's office during the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot. He later studied at Harvard Divinity School, then co-founded and led democracyFIRST, a group focused on countering election denial, serving as executive director from September 2022 to April 2025 before moving back to Maine.

His stated priorities include campaign-finance reform (banning corporate PACs), Medicare for All, a congressional stock-trading ban, capping childcare at $10 per day, an Ultra-Millionaire Tax on wealth over $50 million, and abortion rights. His campaign slogan is "Maine needs courage, not concern." (T2 reporting; T3 campaign material.)

2 Reform Brand vs. Mothership Strategies

Finding 2.1: Reform Brand Against Ties to His Husband's Fundraising Firm HIGH

What happened
Wood campaigns on removing large money from politics, yet his career is entangled with Mothership Strategies, a small-dollar email fundraising firm operated by his husband, Jake Lipsett. FEC records show that after Wood stepped down from democracyFIRST to run against Collins, his former PAC directed more than $500,000 to Mothership. The reporting documents a dollar flow to a spousal financial interest that runs against the reform message.
Campaign response
The campaign frames its money as grassroots, describing it as "built by Mainers," and presents the small-dollar model as a response to corporate PAC influence rather than a contradiction of it.

3 PAC He Led Gave to Republican Candidates

Finding 3.1: democracyFIRST Donated to Republican House and Senate Candidates MODERATE

What happened
democracyFIRST, the PAC Wood led, donated roughly $75,000 to Republican House and Senate candidates in the 2024 cycle. Named recipients include Sen. John Curtis (R-UT), Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE), and Rep. David Valadao (R-CA), plus $5,000 to the moderate Republican Governance Group PAC. The contributions are documented in FEC filings.
Campaign response
Wood says the PAC deliberately backed Republicans with direct authority over election administration or certification who faced election-denier primary challengers, describing it as a firewall to safeguard future elections.

4 Out-of-State Donor Base

Finding 4.1: Donor Base Against the "Built by Mainers" Framing MODERATE

What happened
The campaign's own July 2026 relaunch numbers cite more than 70,000 contributors and about 140,000 donations, but only "over 2,800 Mainers." That implies the large majority of donors are out of state, which sits against the "built by Mainers, for Mainers" framing. The figures are stated from the campaign's own release alongside FEC totals, not as a fundraising characterization.
Campaign response
The campaign presents the national small-dollar base as evidence of broad grassroots support and consistency with its no-corporate-PAC pledge.

5 Office-Switching Timeline

Finding 5.1: Senate to House to Senate Within One Cycle MODERATE

What happened
Within roughly nine months Wood ran for three positions in sequence: he announced a US Senate run on September 30, 2025; switched to the 2nd Congressional District US House primary and filed qualifying signatures on March 9, 2026; lost that primary on June 19, 2026; and re-entered the US Senate race on July 9, 2026. The sequence is documented by contemporaneous reporting.
Campaign response
The campaign frames the moves as responses to a changing field, including Golden's open House seat and the later opening in the Senate contest.
