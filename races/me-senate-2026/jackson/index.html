Executive Summary

Troy Dale Jackson is a fifth-generation Allagash logger and former Maine Senate President (2018 to 2024) who is one of several candidates for the Maine Democratic Party US Senate nomination. The nominee will be chosen by a state convention on July 25, 2026, after Graham Platner suspended his campaign. 1 Background & Record

Jackson is a fifth-generation logger from Allagash who entered politics through the 1998 logging blockade at Maine and Canada border crossings, organized to protest the hiring of Canadian temporary workers over Maine loggers. He was educated at Allagash Consolidated Community High School and the University of Maine at Fort Kent (A.A., Business). His spouse is Lana Pelletier.

He first won office in the Maine House of Representatives in 2002 as an Independent, joined the Democratic Party in 2004, and was re-elected in 2004 and 2006. He served in the Maine Senate from 2008 until December 2024, when he left due to term limits. He held Senate leadership roles including Majority Leader (2013 to 2014) and Minority Leader (2016 to 2018), and served as Senate President from December 2018 to December 2024, succeeded by Mattie Daughtry.

In 2014 he lost the 2nd District Democratic US House primary to Emily Cain. In 2026 he ran for the Democratic gubernatorial nomination, finishing third and being eliminated on the third ranked-choice count; Hannah Pingree won that nomination. Jackson has built his politics on labor and worker-protection issues, including a 2010 bill barring the Maine Department of Conservation from employing foreign laborers at state-owned logging sites, which passed both chambers and was vetoed by Governor Paul LePage. 2 Position Reversals

Jackson holds current positions on two social issues that differ from his earlier record. Both the earlier votes or statements and the current positions are on the public record. Jackson and supporters frame his career as non-ideological and rooted in labor and rural constituent service.

Finding 2.1: Voted Against Same-Sex Marriage in 2009, Now States Support for LGBTQ Rights MODERATE

What happened
In 2009 Jackson voted against the bill to legalize same-sex marriage in Maine. He now states support for LGBTQ rights.

Finding 2.2: Identified as Anti-Abortion in 2012, Now States Support for Abortion Rights MODERATE

What happened
In 2012 Jackson identified as anti-abortion, favoring illegality except in cases of rape or incest, and attributed the view to his Catholic upbringing. He now states support for abortion rights and campaigns on that position.

4 Convention Standing

The Democratic nominee will be chosen by a state convention on July 25, 2026, rather than by the June primary, because Platner suspended his campaign after the primary. The declared field includes Jackson, Dan Kleban of Maine Beer Company, Secretary of State Shenna Bellows, Jordan Wood, and Nirav Shah.

Jackson has reported endorsements from Our Revolution and Representative Ro Khanna (D-CA) and drew labor-union support during his 2026 gubernatorial run. Senator Bernie Sanders headlined events with Jackson during the cycle. The nominee is not yet chosen, and this section is provided as neutral context on the state of the contest as of the pull date.
