Finding 5.1: Presided over the Manuel Ellis officers' trial that ended in acquittals HIGH

What happened
Chushcoff presided over State v. Collins, Burbank and Rankine, the 2023 trial of three Tacoma police officers charged in the March 3, 2020 death of Manuel Ellis. It was the first case tried under the Washington Attorney General's independent-prosecution authority and one of the most closely watched police-accountability trials in state history. On December 21, 2023 the jury acquitted all three officers on all charges. During the trial Chushcoff issued a series of pretrial and evidentiary rulings that the Ellis family and the prosecution said favored the defense, and coverage noted specific evidence kept from jurors. Because he is now running to be the county's chief prosecutor, his handling of that trial is part of the record voters will weigh.

Defense
A trial judge's rulings apply the rules of evidence and constitutional protections regardless of public sentiment, and the verdict was returned by a jury, not the judge. The Attorney General's office, not Chushcoff, prosecuted the case. No appellate court has found that his rulings were legal error, and presiding fairly over a high-profile case, including protecting a defendant's rights, is the core function of a judge. Chushcoff points to 27 years on the bench, including four years running the state's second largest trial court, as evidence of his handling of difficult cases.
